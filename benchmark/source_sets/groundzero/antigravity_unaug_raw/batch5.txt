### Solution for Question GZ41

#### 1. First-Principles Optical Constant ($K$)
The optical constant for static light scattering (SLS) is defined as:
$$K = \frac{4\pi^2 n^2 (dn/dc)^2}{N_A \lambda_0^4}$$
Given constants:
- Solvent refractive index $n = 1.333$
- Refractive index increment $dn/dc = 0.140\text{ mL/g} = 0.140\text{ cm}^3\text{/g}$
- Laser vacuum wavelength $\lambda_0 = 632.8\text{ nm} = 6.328 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}$
- Avogadro's number $N_A = 6.02214 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$
- Monomer molar mass $M_1 = 625\text{ g/mol}$

Calculating $K$:
$$K = \frac{4 \pi^2 (1.333)^2 (0.140\text{ cm}^3/\text{g})^2}{(6.02214 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1})(6.328 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm})^4} = 1.42383 \times 10^{-7}\text{ cm}^2\text{ mol/g}^2$$

#### 2. Debye Plot Linear Regression
The single-angle Debye equation is:
$$\frac{K c}{\Delta R} = \frac{1}{M_w} + 2 A_2 c$$
Using the experimental data points:
- $c = 0.002\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 1.847 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies \frac{K c}{\Delta R} = 1.54178 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
- $c = 0.004\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 3.601 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies \frac{K c}{\Delta R} = 1.58160 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
- $c = 0.006\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 5.269 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies \frac{K c}{\Delta R} = 1.62137 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
- $c = 0.008\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 6.856 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies \frac{K c}{\Delta R} = 1.66142 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
- $c = 0.010\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 8.368 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies \frac{K c}{\Delta R} = 1.70152 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
- $c = 0.012\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 9.811 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies \frac{K c}{\Delta R} = 1.74152 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$

Performing linear least-squares regression of $\frac{K c}{\Delta R}$ against $c$:
- Slope: $2 A_2 = 1.9978 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol mL/g}^2 \implies A_2 = 9.989 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol mL/g}^2$
- Intercept: $\frac{1}{M_w} = 1.501685 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$ ($R^2 = 0.999997$)

From the intercept:
$$M_w = \frac{1}{1.501685 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}} = 66,592\text{ g/mol} \approx 66,600\text{ g/mol}$$
Aggregation number:
$$N_{\text{agg}} = \frac{M_w}{M_1} = \frac{66,592}{625} \approx 106.55 \approx 106.6$$

#### 3. Consistency with Literature
Typical nonionic surfactant micelles of this molecular weight (e.g. polyoxyethylene alkyl/aryl ethers like Triton X-100 or $C_{12}E_{10}$, $M_1 \approx 625\text{ g/mol}$) form spherical or compact oblate micelles in water at ambient temperatures with aggregation numbers characteristic of nonionics ($N_{\text{agg}} \sim 80 - 150$, clustering around $100 - 140$). The measured value of $N_{\text{agg}} \approx 106.6$ falls squarely within this well-documented literature range. Furthermore, the positive second virial coefficient ($A_2 \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol mL/g}^2$) confirms net repulsive, steric/excluded-volume intermicellar interactions in a good solvent.

### Solution for Question GZ42

#### 1. Compound Identity & Curvature Behavior
- **SMILES:** `CCCCC(CC)COC(=O)CC(C(=O)OCC(CC)CCCC)S(=O)(=O)[O-].[Na+]`
- This is sodium bis(2-ethylhexyl) sulfosuccinate (commonly known as AOT or Aerosol OT).
- AOT possesses two bulky, branched 8-carbon tails (2-ethylhexyl groups) attached to a compact sulfosuccinate polar headgroup.
- Because of this twin-branched architecture, AOT has a critical packing parameter $P = v / (a_0 l_c) > 1$ ($P \approx 1.1 - 1.3$), imparting a powerful, natural preference for negative/reverse curvature (water-in-oil microemulsions and reverse micelles) spontaneously without requiring any cosurfactant.

#### 2. Characteristic Curvature ($C_c$) Conventions & Assessment
- In the HLD-NAC framework:
  $$\text{HLD} = \ln(S) - k \cdot \text{EACN} - \alpha \Delta T + C_c$$
  where a positive $C_c$ ($C_c > 0$) promotes positive HLD (Winsor II, oil-continuous / reverse curvature), while a negative $C_c$ ($C_c < 0$) promotes negative HLD (Winsor I, water-continuous / normal curvature).

In fact, $C_c = -0.92$ is a known citation error in secondary literature where AOT was conflated with sodium dihexyl sulfosuccinate (SDHS).
- **Source 2 ($C_c \in [2.4, 3.5]$):** A strongly positive $C_c$ ($+2.4$ to $+3.5$) aligns with AOT's lipophilic preference for reverse curvature. Furthermore, direct experimental determinations reported in a primary paper investigating interfacial and phase behavior are far more reliable than secondary review compilations.

#### 2. Tanford First-Principles Geometric Estimation

- Tail volume:
  $$V_{\text{tail}} = 27.4 + 26.9 \times n_c = 27.4 + 26.9 \times 12 = 350.2\text{ \AA}^3$$
- Critical chain length:
  $$l_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times n_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times 12 = 16.68\text{ \AA}$$

### Solution for Question GZ45

#### 1. Physical Behavior of Ionic Micelles
- **SMILES:** `CCCCCCCCCCCOS(=O)(=O)[O-].[Na+]`
- This is sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), a classic anionic surfactant with a CMC of $\approx 8.3\text{ mM}$ in pure water at 25 °C.
- In ionic surfactants, micellar aggregation number ($N_{\text{agg}}$) is not a fixed invariant material property. Above the CMC, $N_{\text{agg}}$ depends strongly on total surfactant concentration and solution ionic strength.

#### 2. Resolving the Concentration Difference
- **Near-CMC regime ($c \sim 8 - 15\text{ mM}$):** Most broader literature surveys report $N_{\text{agg}}$ determined close to the CMC (in the range $44.8 - 54.2$, mean $\approx 49.5$). Near the CMC, the ionic strength is low, and strong electrostatic repulsion between adjacent charged sulfate headgroups maximizes the effective area per headgroup ($a_0 \approx 62 - 65\text{ \AA}^2$). This keeps the critical packing parameter $P = v / (a_0 l_c) \le 1/3$, constraining the micelle to a minimum spherical aggregation number ($N_{\text{agg}} \approx 50 - 55$).
- **Higher concentration regime ($c = 69\text{ mM}$):** At $69\text{ mM}$ (more than 8 times the CMC), there is $\approx 61\text{ mM}$ of micellized surfactant and an accompanying dense cloud of $\text{Na}^+$ counterions. The elevated ionic strength significantly screens headgroup electrostatic repulsion, shrinking the effective headgroup area $a_0$. As $a_0$ decreases, the packing parameter $P$ increases toward the sphere-to-cylinder transition boundary ($P > 1/3$), driving micellar growth and slight prolate/rodlike elongation with $N_{\text{agg}}$ increasing to $60 - 80$.
- Therefore, the $69\text{ mM}$ measurement ($N_{\text{agg}} = 63$) does not genuinely conflict with the near-CMC literature range ($44.8 - 54.2$); both are physically consistent realizations at different ionic strengths.

### Solution for Question GZ46

#### 1. Governing Equation & Parameters
- Surfactant: Sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS)
- Characteristic curvature: $C_c = -2.63$
- Salinity sensitivity constant: $k = 0.16$
- Optimum salinity relation (HLD-NAC):
  $$S^* = \exp(k \cdot \text{EACN} - C_c)$$
  where $S^*$ is expressed in equivalent $\text{g NaCl / 100 mL}$ ($\text{wt}\%$) of brine.

#### 2. Calculations for Real Oils
1. **Toluene ($\text{EACN} = 0$):**
   $$k \cdot \text{EACN} - C_c = 0.16 \times 0 - (-2.63) = 2.63$$
   $$S^*_{\text{toluene}} = \exp(2.63) \approx 13.874\text{ g/100 mL} \approx 13.87\%$$
   - Physical achievability: Highly achievable. $13.87\text{ g/100 mL}$ is well below the aqueous solubility limit of $\text{NaCl}$ in water at 25 °C ($\approx 26.4\text{ wt}\%$ or $\approx 31\text{ g/100 mL}$).

2. **Hexadecane ($\text{EACN} = 16$):**
   $$k \cdot \text{EACN} - C_c = 0.16 \times 16 - (-2.63) = 2.56 + 2.63 = 5.19$$
   $$S^*_{\text{hexadecane}} = \exp(5.19) \approx 179.467\text{ g/100 mL} \approx 179.47\%$$
   - Physical achievability: Unachievable in practice. $179.47\text{ g/100 mL}$ exceeds the saturation limit of $\text{NaCl}$ in water by roughly a factor of 6.

#### 3. Physical Implication
Because SDS has a strongly negative characteristic curvature ($C_c = -2.63$, highly hydrophilic), it cannot balance an oil as hydrophobic as hexadecane using $\text{NaCl}$ alone without salt precipitating. Reaching a Winsor III microemulsion with hexadecane requires a more lipophilic surfactant (higher $C_c$), an extended surfactant, or a lipophilic cosurfactant (such as a medium-chain alcohol).
