### Solution for Question GZ21

#### 1. Geometric Dimensions from Tanford Relations
For a saturated, unbranched alkyl chain with $n_c = 12$ carbons:
- **Tail Volume ($V$):**
  $$V = 27.4 + 26.9 \times n_c = 27.4 + 26.9 \times 12 = 350.2\text{ \AA}^3$$
- **Critical Chain Length ($l_c$):**
  $$l_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times n_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times 12 = 16.68\text{ \AA}$$

#### 2. Directly Measured Headgroup Area Approach
Given the independently measured headgroup area $a = 59.62\text{ \AA}^2$:
- **Critical Packing Parameter ($P$):**
  $$P = \frac{V}{a \times l_c} = \frac{350.2}{59.62 \times 16.68} = \frac{350.2}{994.4616} \approx 0.35215$$
- **Morphology Classification:**
  Since $1/3 \approx 0.3333 < P \le 1/2$, this corresponds to a **cylindrical/rodlike micelle**.

#### 3. Electrostatically-Derived Approach
For an ionic surfactant in water without added salt, estimating the effective headgroup area from electrostatic double-layer theory requires:
1. The Debye screening length $\kappa^{-1}$ at the ionic strength of the CMC ($I \approx 12.93\text{ mM}$).
2. An equilibrium headgroup area relation $a_e = C_{\text{prefactor}} / \sqrt{1 + l_c/\kappa^{-1}}$.

However, the electrostatic prefactor $C_{\text{prefactor}}$ is strictly headgroup-specific (for instance, $82.0\text{ \AA}$ is calibrated specifically for anionic sulfate headgroups like SDS). For a cationic quaternary ammonium headgroup ($-\text{N}^+(\text{CH}_3)_3$), no validated electrostatic prefactor is available in this toolkit or established literature. Substituting a prefactor from an unrelated headgroup class (such as sulfate) is physically unjustified. Therefore, the electrostatically-derived area cannot be determined and must be reported as `null`.

  $$\text{HLB} = -0.27 \times (-5.7) + 20.1 = 1.539 + 20.1 = 21.639$$

#### 2. Compound 2 Classification and HLB
- **SMILES:** `CCCCCCCCCCCCN`

  The conjugate acid of an aliphatic primary amine ($\text{RNH}_3^+$) has an aqueous $\text{p}K_a$ in the range of $10.4 - 10.8$ (typical mean $\sim 10.6$). By the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:
  $$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log_{10}\left(\frac{[\text{RNH}_2]}{[\text{RNH}_3^+]}\right) \implies 7.0 = 10.6 + \log_{10}\left(\frac{[\text{RNH}_2]}{[\text{RNH}_3^+]}\right)$$

  Because it is $>99.9\%$ protonated at neutral pH, its charge type is **cationic**.
- **HLB via $C_c$ Regression:**

Furthermore, the regression equation ($\text{HLB} = -0.27 \times C_c + 20.1$) was calibrated specifically on 5 quaternary ammonium compounds and does not structurally extend to primary amines. Hence, its HLB via this regression cannot be determined and is reported as `null`.

### Solution for Question GZ23

#### 1. Charge Type and Structural Family
- **SMILES:** `[Br-].[Br-].CCCCCCCCCCCC[N+](C)(C)CCCC[N+](C)(C)CCCCCCCCCCCC`
- **Charge Type:** **cationic**. The molecule contains two quaternary ammonium cation centers ($-\text{N}^+(\text{CH}_3)_2-$) counterbalanced by two bromide anions ($2\text{ Br}^-$).
- **Structural Family:** **dimeric/gemini** (specifically a 12-4-12 bis-quaternary ammonium gemini surfactant). The structure consists of two identical dodecyl (C12) hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails attached to two cationic dimethylammonium headgroups, which are covalently joined together at the headgroup level by a flexible tetramethylene (butyl, C4) hydrocarbon spacer.

#### 2. HLB Estimation Method Evaluation
- **Davies Method:** **Invalid**. Davies' method is an empirical additive scheme that sums group numbers for independent lipophilic and hydrophilic moieties. For a gemini surfactant, the central polymethylene spacer linking the two headgroups is held at the aqueous interface under strong geometric and electrostatic constraints, behaving entirely differently from a free terminal alkyl chain. These strong intra- and intermolecular interactions violate the linear additivity assumption, making Davies' group contribution method structurally invalid for geminis.
- **Griffin Method:** **Valid**. Griffin's method computes HLB strictly from the mass ratio of hydrophilic to total molecular mass:
  $$\text{HLB} = 20 \times \frac{M_{\text{hydrophilic}}}{M_{\text{total}}}$$
  Because Griffin's method does not assume non-interacting, additive conformational freedom of individual chain segments, it remains physically and structurally applicable to gemini architectures.

### Solution for Question GZ24

#### 1. Binary Mixed Micelle Analysis via Rubingh's Model
- **Component 1:** Sodium cholate (NaCA), pure $\text{CMC}_1 = 11.50\text{ mM}$.
- **Component 2:** Sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), pure $\text{CMC}_2 = 11.98\text{ mM}$.
- **Bulk Mole Fractions:** $\alpha_2 = 0.5 \implies \alpha_1 = 0.5$.
- **Measured Mixed CMC:** $\text{CMC}_{\text{mix}} = 4.07\text{ mM}$ at $T = 298.15\text{ K}$.

Solving Rubingh's transcendental equation for micellar mole fraction $x_1$:
$$\frac{x_1^2 \ln\left(\frac{\alpha_1 \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}}}{x_1 \text{CMC}_1}\right)}{(1 - x_1)^2 \ln\left(\frac{(1 - \alpha_1) \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}}}{(1 - x_1) \text{CMC}_2}\right)} = 1$$
Substituting $\alpha_1 = 0.5$, $\text{CMC}_{\text{mix}} = 4.07$, $\text{CMC}_1 = 11.50$, $\text{CMC}_2 = 11.98$:
$$x_1 \approx 0.5033$$
Evaluating Rubingh's interaction parameter $\beta$:
$$\beta = \frac{\ln\left(\frac{\alpha_1 \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}}}{x_1 \text{CMC}_1}\right)}{(1 - x_1)^2} = \frac{\ln\left(\frac{0.5 \times 4.07}{0.5033 \times 11.50}\right)}{(1 - 0.5033)^2} = \frac{\ln(0.3516)}{(0.4967)^2} \approx -4.236$$

#### 2. Characterization of Interaction
The interaction parameter $\beta \approx -4.24$ is large and negative, indicating **strong synergism / attractive interaction** between the planar rigid bile salt steroid skeleton and the flexible aliphatic alkyl sulfate chain, promoting mixed micelle formation well below the ideal solution prediction ($\text{CMC}_{\text{ideal}} \approx 11.73\text{ mM}$).

#### 3. Applicability of Maeda's Free Energy Expression
Maeda's mixed micelle free energy model:
$$\Delta G_M = R T (B_0 + B_1 X_1 + B_2 X_1^2)$$
was derived specifically for **ionic-nonionic surfactant mixtures**, explicitly accounting for the electrostatic stabilization that occurs when neutral headgroups insert between ionic headgroups to dilute surface charge density. In this system, both sodium cholate and SDS are **anionic**. Applying Maeda's model to an anionic-anionic pair represents a domain violation. While plugging values into the formula yields an apparent value of $\approx -23.60\text{ kJ/mol}$, the model is theoretically inappropriate for this system, so the value is reported as `null`.

Counterion dissociation degree $\alpha = 0.268$.
Water molarity = $55.5\text{ mol/L}$.
The mole fraction of surfactant at the CMC is:
$$X_{\text{cmc}} = \frac{C_{\text{cmc}}}{55.5 + C_{\text{cmc}}}$$

Data points:
- $T = 283.15\text{ K}: C = 9.93\text{ mM} \implies X_{\text{cmc}} = 1.7889 \times 10^{-4}$
- $T = 288.15\text{ K}: C = 9.82\text{ mM} \implies X_{\text{cmc}} = 1.7691 \times 10^{-4}$
- $T = 293.15\text{ K}: C = 9.76\text{ mM} \implies X_{\text{cmc}} = 1.7583 \times 10^{-4}$

- $T = 303.15\text{ K}: C = 9.77\text{ mM} \implies X_{\text{cmc}} = 1.7601 \times 10^{-4}$
- $T = 308.15\text{ K}: C = 10.02\text{ mM} \implies X_{\text{cmc}} = 1.8051 \times 10^{-4}$
- $T = 313.15\text{ K}: C = 10.54\text{ mM} \implies X_{\text{cmc}} = 1.8988 \times 10^{-4}$
- $T = 318.15\text{ K}: C = 11.47\text{ mM} \implies X_{\text{cmc}} = 2.0662 \times 10^{-4}$

Two-point van 't Hoff enthalpy:
$$\Delta H_{\text{mic}} = R \frac{\ln(X_{\text{cmc},2}) - \ln(X_{\text{cmc},1})}{\frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_1}}$$

#### 2. Enthalpy Evaluations
- **(a) Widest Available Span ($283.15\text{ K}$ to $318.15\text{ K}$):**
  $$\Delta H_{\text{wide}} = 8.31446 \times \frac{\ln(2.0662 \times 10^{-4}) - \ln(1.7889 \times 10^{-4})}{\frac{1}{318.15} - \frac{1}{283.15}} \times 10^{-3} \approx -3.085\text{ kJ/mol}$$
- **(b) Low Temperature Regime ($283.15\text{ K}$ to $298.15\text{ K}$):**
  Here CMC decreases with increasing temperature (hydrophobic dehydration dominant):
  $$\Delta H_{\text{low}} = 8.31446 \times \frac{\ln(1.7547 \times 10^{-4}) - \ln(1.7889 \times 10^{-4})}{\frac{1}{298.15} - \frac{1}{283.15}} \times 10^{-3} \approx +0.904\text{ kJ/mol}$$
- **(c) High Temperature Regime ($303.15\text{ K}$ to $318.15\text{ K}$):**
  Here CMC increases with increasing temperature (headgroup repulsion / thermal disruption dominant):
  $$\Delta H_{\text{high}} = 8.31446 \times \frac{\ln(2.0662 \times 10^{-4}) - \ln(1.7601 \times 10^{-4})}{\frac{1}{318.15} - \frac{1}{303.15}} \times 10^{-3} \approx -8.574\text{ kJ/mol}$$

#### 3. Self-Consistency and Free Energy
- **Self-Consistency:** `false`. The low-temperature enthalpy is endothermic ($+0.904\text{ kJ/mol}$) whereas the high-temperature enthalpy is exothermic ($-8.574\text{ kJ/mol}$). The CMC passes through a minimum near $298\text{ K}$, demonstrating a substantial heat capacity change ($\Delta C_p > 0$) that causes the constant-$\Delta H$ assumption to completely break down.
- **Gibbs Free Energy of Micellization at $298.15\text{ K}$:**
  With counterion factor $(2 - \alpha) = 2 - 0.268 = 1.732$:
  $$\Delta G_{\text{mic}} = (2 - \alpha) R T \ln(X_{\text{cmc}}) = 1.732 \times 8.31446 \times 298.15 \times \ln(1.7547 \times 10^{-4}) \times 10^{-3} \approx -37.131\text{ kJ/mol}$$

### Solution for Question GZ27

#### 1. Packing Parameter and Micelle Morphology
For sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS, $n_c = 12$):
- $V = 27.4 + 26.9 \times 12 = 350.2\text{ \AA}^3$
- $l_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times 12 = 16.68\text{ \AA}$
- $a = 44.70\text{ \AA}^2$
- **Critical Packing Parameter:**
  $$P = \frac{V}{a \times l_c} = \frac{350.2}{44.70 \times 16.68} \approx 0.4697$$
- **Morphology:** Since $1/3 < P \le 1/2$, the morphology is a **cylindrical/rodlike micelle**.

#### 2. Prolate Ellipsoidal Core Axial Ratio
Given aggregation number $N_{\text{agg}} = 63$:
- Core volume $V_{\text{core}} = 63 \times 350.2 = 22062.6\text{ \AA}^3$.
- Minor semi-axis $b = l_c = 16.68\text{ \AA}$.
- For a prolate ellipsoid of revolution:
  $$V_{\text{core}} = \frac{4}{3} \pi a_{\text{maj}} b^2 \implies a_{\text{maj}} = \frac{3 \times 22062.6}{4 \pi \times (16.68)^2} = \frac{66187.8}{3496.26} \approx 18.931\text{ \AA}$$
- **Axial Ratio:**
  $$\frac{a_{\text{maj}}}{b} = \frac{18.931}{16.68} \approx 1.135$$

#### 3. Hydrodynamic Radius and Perrin Correction
Given $D = 9.3 \times 10^{-7}\text{ cm}^2/\text{s} = 9.3 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$, $\eta = 0.8903\text{ mPa}\cdot\text{s} = 8.903 \times 10^{-4}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, $T = 298.15\text{ K}$:
- **Naive Stokes-Einstein Hydrodynamic Radius ($R_H^{\text{naive}}$):**
  $$R_H^{\text{naive}} = \frac{k_B T}{6 \pi \eta D} = \frac{1.380649 \times 10^{-23} \times 298.15}{6 \pi \times 8.903 \times 10^{-4} \times 9.3 \times 10^{-11}} \approx 2.6375 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m} = 2.638\text{ nm}$$
- **Perrin Friction Factor ($F_P$):**
  For axial ratio $p = 1.135$:
  $$\xi = \frac{\sqrt{p^2 - 1}}{p} = \frac{\sqrt{1.135^2 - 1}}{1.135} \approx 0.4725$$
  $$S = \frac{2 \operatorname{arctanh}(\xi)}{\xi} = \frac{2 \times 0.5134}{0.4725} \approx 2.1731$$
  $$F_P = \frac{2 p^{2/3}}{S} = \frac{2 \times (1.135)^{2/3}}{2.1731} \approx 1.0014$$
- **True (Shape-Corrected) Hydrodynamic Radius ($R_{\text{eff}}$):**
  $$R_{\text{eff}} = \frac{R_H^{\text{naive}}}{F_P} = \frac{2.6375}{1.0014} \approx 2.634\text{ nm}$$
- **Direction of Correction and Rationale:**
  The shape correction makes the size estimate **smaller** than the naive Stokes-Einstein value. An elongated prolate particle experiences greater translational hydrodynamic drag than a sphere of equal volume ($F_P > 1$). Because the naive Stokes-Einstein equation assumes sphericity, it mistakenly attributes this extra shape-induced friction to a larger particle radius. Correcting for shape removes this overestimation, resulting in a smaller true equivalent-sphere hydrodynamic radius.
