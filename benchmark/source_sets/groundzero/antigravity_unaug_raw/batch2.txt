### Solution for Question GZ11

#### 1. Geometric Dimensions from Tanford Relations
For a saturated, unbranched alkyl chain with $n_c = 12$ carbons:
- **Tail Volume ($V$):**
  $$V = 27.4 + 26.9 \times n_c = 27.4 + 26.9 \times 12 = 350.2\text{ \AA}^3$$
- **Critical Chain Length ($l_c$):**
  $$l_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times n_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times 12 = 16.68\text{ \AA}$$

#### 2. Approach A: Directly Measured Headgroup Area
Given $a_{\text{meas}} = 44.70\text{ \AA}^2$:
- **Critical Packing Parameter ($P_{\text{meas}}$):**
  $$P_{\text{meas}} = \frac{V}{a_{\text{meas}} \times l_c} = \frac{350.2}{44.70 \times 16.68} = \frac{350.2}{745.596} \approx 0.46969$$
- **Morphology Classification:**
  Since $1/3 < P \le 1/2$, this corresponds to a **cylindrical/rodlike micelle**.

#### 3. Approach B: Electrostatically-Derived Equilibrium Headgroup Area
For an ionic surfactant in water with no added electrolyte, the ionic strength at the CMC is governed primarily by the singly dispersed surfactant ions: $I \approx \text{CMC} = 8\text{ mM} = 0.008\text{ M}$.
- **Debye-Hückel Screening Length ($\kappa^{-1}$):**
  Using the Debye-Hückel relation for water at 298.15 K ($\epsilon = 80.0$):
  $$\kappa^{-1} = \left( \frac{8 \pi N_A e^2 (\text{CMC})}{1000 \, \epsilon k_B T} \right)^{-1/2} \approx 34.335\text{ \AA}$$
- **Equilibrium Area per Headgroup ($a_e$):**
  With the sulfate headgroup-specific electrostatic prefactor of $82.0\text{ \AA}$:
  $$a_e = \frac{82.0}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{l_c}{\kappa^{-1}}}} = \frac{82.0}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{16.68}{34.335}}} = \frac{82.0}{\sqrt{1.4858}} \approx 67.272\text{ \AA}^2$$
- **Critical Packing Parameter ($P_{\text{es}}$):**
  $$P_{\text{es}} = \frac{V}{a_e \times l_c} = \frac{350.2}{67.272 \times 16.68} = \frac{350.2}{1122.09} \approx 0.31210$$
- **Morphology Classification:**
  Since $P \le 1/3$, this corresponds to a **spherical micelle**.

#### 4. Physical Commentary
The two approaches predict different aggregate morphologies: the planar surface tension measurement ($a = 44.70\text{ \AA}^2$) yields $P \approx 0.470$ (cylindrical/rodlike), whereas electrostatic double-layer theory at the CMC ($a_e \approx 67.27\text{ \AA}^2$) yields $P \approx 0.312$ (spherical). This reflects a genuine physical tension between different methods: measured $A_{\min}$ reflects dense packing at a flat air-water interface, whereas the electrostatic model accounts for strong unshielded electrostatic repulsion between sulfate headgroups at low ionic strength (8 mM salt-free) on a curved micellar surface, expanding the effective headgroup area and favoring spherical micelles near the CMC.

### Solution for Question GZ12

#### 1. Analysis of Binary Mixed Micelle Interaction
The measured activity coefficients across the composition series are:
- $x_1 = 0.305: f_1 = 0.394, f_2 = 0.650$
- $x_1 = 0.415: f_1 = 0.630, f_2 = 0.500$
- $x_1 = 0.483: f_1 = 0.769, f_2 = 0.426$
- $x_1 = 0.626: f_1 = 0.970, f_2 = 0.322$
- $x_1 = 0.808: f_1 = 1.028, f_2 = 0.286$

Notice the pronounced asymmetry:
- In symmetric Rubingh Regular Solution Theory (RST), $\ln f_1 = \beta (1-x_1)^2$ and $\ln f_2 = \beta x_1^2$. If $\beta < 0$, both $f_1$ and $f_2$ must remain strictly $< 1.0$ at all compositions.
- In this system, however, as $x_1$ increases, $f_1$ approaches and exceeds unity ($1.028$ at $x_1 = 0.808$), while $f_2$ remains strongly suppressed ($0.286$).
- This structural asymmetry arises from the difference between Component 1 (Hyamine / benzethonium chloride: bulky, aromatic, branched) and Component 2 (DTAB: compact linear alkyl chain).

#### 2. Two-Parameter Asymmetric Margules Model (EOMMM)
To accurately describe this system, a two-parameter Margules model is required:
$$g^E / RT = x_1 x_2 (w_{12} x_2 + w_{21} x_1)$$
$$\ln f_1 = x_2^2 [w_{12} + 2(w_{21} - w_{12}) x_1]$$
$$\ln f_2 = x_1^2 [w_{21} + 2(w_{12} - w_{21}) x_2]$$
With fitted parameters $w_{12} = -3.565$ and $w_{21} = -0.889$ (in $RT$ units):
At $x_1 = 0.90$ ($x_2 = 0.10$):
$$\ln f_1 = (0.10)^2 [-3.565 + 2(-0.889 - (-3.565))(0.90)] = 0.01 [-3.565 + 2(2.676)(0.90)] = 0.01 [1.2518] = 0.012518 \implies f_1 \approx 1.0126$$
$$\ln f_2 = (0.90)^2 [-0.889 + 2(-3.565 - (-0.889))(0.10)] = 0.81 [-0.889 + 2(-2.676)(0.10)] = 0.81 [-1.4242] = -1.1536 \implies f_2 \approx 0.3155$$

- Between $0.01585\text{ mM}$ and $1.58489\text{ mM}$ (6 pre-CMC points spanning 2 decades in concentration), the surface tension decreases strictly linearly against $\log_{10} c$:
  $$\frac{d\gamma}{d\log_{10} c} = -16.400\text{ mN/m per decade} \quad (R^2 = 1.000)$$
- This indicates a saturated interfacial monolayer with constant surface excess $\Gamma_{\max}$.

#### 2. Lateral Surfactant-Surfactant Interactions
- Fitting the pre-CMC isotherm to the Frumkin model yields an unconstrained parameter $a \approx 3.79$, which falls in the attractive lateral interaction regime ($a \ge 2$, 2D condensation).
- However, comparing the models via Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC):
  The simpler Langmuir/Szyszkowski model ($a = 0$, independent adsorption with no lateral interaction) yields a lower BIC than the Frumkin model ($\Delta\text{BIC} \approx -12.09$ favoring Langmuir), because the pre-CMC data is virtually indistinguishable from a straight line and does not statistically justify the additional degree of freedom.

#### 3. Confidence Assessment
Because there are only 6 pre-CMC points and the curve is perfectly linear in the logarithmic regime, the data is statistically underdetermined with respect to lateral interactions. The numerical non-zero fit of $a \approx 3.79$ cannot be confirmed against the simpler $a = 0$ model. Therefore, confidence in asserting lateral interaction from this dataset alone is **low**.

### Solution for Question GZ16

#### 1. Compound Classification
SMILES: `CCCCCCCCCCCCOS(=O)(=O)[O-].[Na+]` (Sodium dodecyl sulfate, SDS)
- The amphiphile carries a sulfate ester group ($-\text{OSO}_3^-$) balanced by a sodium cation ($\text{Na}^+$).
- Charge type: **anionic** (1:1 ionic surfactant, $n = 2$ in the absence of added inert salt).

#### 2. Curve Analysis and CMC Determination
A three-segment linear regression (baseline + steep linear decline + plateau) identifies the transition breakpoint:
- Premicellar slope: $\frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C} = -16.876\text{ mN/m}$ ($\frac{d\gamma}{d\log_{10} C} = -38.857\text{ mN/m per decade}$, $R^2 = 1.000$).
- Postmicellar mean plateau: $37.38\text{ mN/m}$.
- Critical micelle concentration: $\text{CMC} = 9.1815\text{ mM} = 0.0091815\text{ M}$.

#### 3. Pre-CMC Adsorption Parameters
Using the Gibbs adsorption isotherm with $n = 2$ at $T = 296.15\text{ K}$:
$$\Gamma_{\max} = -\frac{1}{2 R T} \frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C} = \frac{16.8756 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N/m}}{2 \times 8.31446\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)} \times 296.15\text{ K}} \approx 3.4268 \times 10^{-6}\text{ mol/m}^2$$
$$A_{\min} = \frac{1}{N_A \Gamma_{\max}} = \frac{1}{6.02214 \times 10^{23} \times 3.42675 \times 10^{-6}}\text{ m}^2 \approx 0.4846\text{ nm}^2$$

#### 4. Thermodynamics of Micellization
- With counterion dissociation degree $\alpha = 0.272$:
  $$X_{\text{cmc}} = \frac{0.0091815}{55.5 + 0.0091815} \approx 1.65406 \times 10^{-4}$$
  $$\ln X_{\text{cmc}} \approx -8.7071$$
  $$\Delta G_{\text{mic}}^\circ = (1 + \alpha) R T \ln X_{\text{cmc}} = (1 + 0.272) \times 8.31446 \times 296.15 \times (-8.7071) \approx -27271\text{ J/mol} = -27.271\text{ kJ/mol}$$
- **Entropy of Micellization ($\Delta S_{\text{mic}}^\circ$):**
  Determining $\Delta S_{\text{mic}}^\circ = -\frac{\partial \Delta G_{\text{mic}}^\circ}{\partial T}$ requires temperature-dependent CMC data across multiple temperatures (van't Hoff analysis). Since measurements are provided at only a single temperature (296.15 K), $\Delta S_{\text{mic}}$ cannot genuinely be determined and must be reported as `null`.

### Solution for Question GZ18

#### 1. Critical Packing Parameter and Morphology
For a saturated, unbranched alkyl chain with $n_c = 12$ carbons:
- Tail volume:
  $$V = 27.4 + 26.9 \times 12 = 350.2\text{ \AA}^3$$
- Critical chain length:
  $$l_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times 12 = 16.68\text{ \AA}$$
- Critical packing parameter with $a = 44.70\text{ \AA}^2$:
  $$P = \frac{V}{a \times l_c} = \frac{350.2}{44.70 \times 16.68} \approx 0.4697$$
- Since $1/3 < P \le 1/2$, the predicted morphology is **cylindrical/rodlike micelle**.

#### 2. Prolate Ellipsoid Axial Ratio Calculation
Because the predicted morphology is cylindrical/rodlike, the micelle core can be modeled as a prolate ellipsoid of revolution with volume conservation:
- Minor semi-axis: $b = l_c = 16.68\text{ \AA}$.
- Total core volume for aggregation number $N_{\text{agg}} = 63$:
  $$V_{\text{core}} = N_{\text{agg}} \times V = 63 \times 350.2 = 22062.6\text{ \AA}^3$$
- Prolate ellipsoid volume:
  $$V_{\text{core}} = \frac{4}{3} \pi a b^2 \implies a = \frac{3 V_{\text{core}}}{4 \pi b^2}$$
- Major semi-axis $a$:
  $$a = \frac{3 \times 22062.6}{4 \pi \times (16.68)^2} = \frac{66187.8}{4 \pi \times 278.2224} = \frac{66187.8}{3496.25} \approx 18.931\text{ \AA}$$
- Axial ratio ($a/b$):
  $$\text{axial\_ratio} = \frac{a}{b} = \frac{18.931}{16.68} \approx 1.135$$

- Tail volume per molecule:
  $$V = 27.4 + 26.9 \times 12 = 350.2\text{ \AA}^3/\text{molecule}$$

  $$V_{\text{tail, molar}} = 350.2\text{ \AA}^3/\text{molecule} \times 6.02214 \times 10^{23}\text{ molecules/mol} \times 10^{-24}\text{ cm}^3/\text{\AA}^3 \approx 210.895\text{ cm}^3/\text{mol} \approx 210.9\text{ cm}^3/\text{mol}$$

  $$\bar{v} = \frac{V_{\text{tail, molar}} + V_{\text{head, molar}}}{\text{MW}}$$

- Electrostriction prevents $V_{\text{head, molar}}$ from being derived geometrically. Without an independently measured or literature-sourced partial molar volume for the sulfate headgroup plus sodium counterion in water, the full $\bar{v}$ cannot be calculated and must be reported as `null`.
