### Solution for Question GZ01

#### 1. Surfactant Structure and Charge Classification
The SMILES string:
`CCCCC(CC)COC(=O)CC(C(=O)OCC(CC)CCCC)S(=O)(=O)[O-].[Na+]`
corresponds to sodium bis(2-ethylhexyl) sulfosuccinate (commonly known as Aerosol-OT or AOT). 
- The polar headgroup contains a sulfonate group ($-\text{SO}_3^-$) carrying a permanent negative charge balanced by a sodium cation ($\text{Na}^+$).
- There are no cationic groups in the organic amphiphile.
- Therefore, the surfactant is **anionic** (a 1:1 ionic surfactant). In the absence of added inert electrolyte, the Gibbs adsorption equation prefactor is $n = 2$ ($1 + 1$ for the univalent surfactant ion and its counterion).

#### 2. Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC)
The pre-CMC data points (concentration $c$ in mM, surface tension $\gamma$ in mN/m) show a constant slope against $\log_{10} c$:
- Between $c = 0.01585\text{ mM}$ and $c = 1.58489\text{ mM}$ (spanning exactly $2.0$ decades in $\log_{10} c$ across 5 equal intervals of $\Delta \log_{10} c = 0.40$):
  - $\Delta \gamma = -6.56\text{ mN/m}$ per 0.40 decade $\implies \frac{d\gamma}{d\log_{10} c} = -16.400\text{ mN/m per decade}$.
  - The linear regression of the pre-micellar regime is:
    $$\gamma(c) = 32.700 - 16.400 \log_{10}(c / \text{mM}) \quad (R^2 = 1.000)$$
- Post-CMC data points:
  - $(3.981, 27.5)$, $(6.31, 27.8)$, $(10.0, 28.0)$
  - A two-segment piecewise linear regression (break-point determination minimizing total RSS) accounts for the slight post-micellar slope ($\approx 1.25\text{ mN/m per decade}$, intercept $26.767\text{ mN/m}$) and yields the intersection at:
    $$\log_{10}(c_{\text{CMC}} / \text{mM}) = \frac{32.700 - 26.767}{16.400 + 1.250} \approx 0.4000 \implies c_{\text{CMC}} \approx 2.512\text{ mM}$$
  - (Assuming an idealized flat plateau at the mean post-CMC tension of $27.767\text{ mN/m}$ yields $c \approx 2.00\text{ mM}$, with the two-segment break-point giving $2.512\text{ mM}$).

#### 3. Pre-CMC Adsorption Behavior
In the pre-CMC region, the surface tension decreases linearly with $\ln c$, indicating a saturated interfacial monolayer where the surface excess $\Gamma_{\max}$ is constant.
Using the Gibbs adsorption isotherm for a 1:1 ionic surfactant without added salt ($n = 2$):
$$\Gamma_{\max} = -\frac{1}{2 R T} \frac{d\gamma}{d\ln c} = -\frac{1}{2 R T \ln 10} \frac{d\gamma}{d\log_{10} c}$$
With $T = 298.15\text{ K}$, $R = 8.31446\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$:
$$\frac{d\gamma}{d\ln c} = \frac{-16.400 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N/m}}{\ln 10} \approx -7.1225 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N/m}$$
$$\Gamma_{\max} = \frac{7.1225 \times 10^{-3}}{2 \times 8.31446 \times 298.15} \approx 1.4366 \times 10^{-6}\text{ mol/m}^2$$
The minimum area per molecule $A_{\min}$ at the interface is:
$$A_{\min} = \frac{1}{N_A \Gamma_{\max}} = \frac{1}{6.02214 \times 10^{23} \times 1.43659 \times 10^{-6}}\text{ m}^2 \approx 1.156 \times 10^{-18}\text{ m}^2 = 1.156\text{ nm}^2$$
Fitting the curve to Langmuir vs Frumkin adsorption isotherms yields a winning BIC for the Langmuir/Szyszkowski model over Frumkin, confirming that lateral interactions at this interface do not warrant an additional parameter given the pre-CMC linearity.

#### 4. Free Energy of Micellization
For an ionic surfactant, the standard Gibbs free energy of micellization depends critically on the degree of counterion binding $\beta$ (or counterion dissociation fraction $\alpha = 1 - \beta$):
$$\Delta G_{\text{mic}}^\circ = (1 + \beta) R T \ln X_{\text{CMC}} \quad \text{or} \quad (2 - \alpha) R T \ln X_{\text{CMC}}$$
Because no independent measurement of counterion binding (such as conductometry slope ratio or potentiometry) is provided, $\beta$ is completely undetermined from surface tension alone. Therefore, $\Delta G_{\text{mic}}^\circ$ cannot genuinely be determined without making an arbitrary assumption about $\beta$, and must be reported as `null`.

The pure component CMCs are $C_1 = 1.8\text{ mM}$ and $C_2 = 8.3\text{ mM}$.

- $\alpha_2 = 0.95, C_{\text{mix}} = 3.6\text{ mM}$
- $\alpha_2 = 0.90, C_{\text{mix}} = 2.8\text{ mM}$
- $\alpha_2 = 0.80, C_{\text{mix}} = 1.8\text{ mM}$
- $\alpha_2 = 0.60, C_{\text{mix}} = 1.4\text{ mM}$
- $\alpha_2 = 0.20, C_{\text{mix}} = 1.3\text{ mM}$

Under Clint's ideal solution theory:
$$\frac{1}{C_{\text{mix}}^{\text{ideal}}} = \frac{\alpha_1}{C_1} + \frac{\alpha_2}{C_2} = \frac{1 - \alpha_2}{1.8} + \frac{\alpha_2}{8.3}$$

- Fitting the extended asymmetric two-parameter Margules model (EOMMM / asymmetric Margules: $g^E / RT = x_1 x_2 (W_{21} x_1 + W_{12} x_2)$) yields parameters:

  with $\text{BIC} \approx -26.04$, which provides a modest improvement in BIC over symmetric Rubingh theory by capturing the composition dependence of the interaction between the bulky zwitterionic headgroup and the compact anionic headgroup.

Because the interaction parameters are strongly negative ($\beta < 0$ and $|\beta| > |\ln(C_1 / C_2)| = |\ln(1.8 / 8.3)| \approx 1.53$) and the mixed CMC drops below both pure-surfactant CMCs, the system exhibits strong attractive **synergy** (synergistic).

Using the model-independent Rodenas relationship:
$$x_1^M = \alpha_1 - \alpha_1(1 - \alpha_1) \frac{d\ln C_{\text{mix}}}{d\alpha_1}$$
differentiating the experimental $C_{\text{mix}}(\alpha_1)$ curve directly yields micellar mole fractions $x_1^M$:
- Across the composition series ($\alpha_1 = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40, 0.80$), $x_1^M$ evaluates to approximately $[0.298, 0.534, 0.738, 0.616, 0.715]$.
- Because $x_1^M > \alpha_1$ across the entire range, Component 1 (DHPC) is strongly preferentially incorporated into the mixed micelles over Component 2. This model-independent check confirms favorable attractive interactions and synergistic mixing without relying on regular solution activity coefficient assumptions.

### Solution for Question GZ03

#### 1. Charge Type and Structure
SMILES: `[Br-].[Br-].CCCCCCCCCCCCCCCC[N+](C)(C)CCCCCC[N+](C)(C)CCCCCCCCCCCCCCCC`
- The amphiphilic cation contains two quaternary ammonium nitrogen atoms, each carrying a permanent $+1$ formal charge: $-\text{N}^+(\text{CH}_3)_2-$.
- There are two bromide ($\text{Br}^-$) counterions.
- Thus, the surfactant is **cationic** (specifically, dicationic / bis-quaternary ammonium).

#### 2. Structural Family
- The molecule consists of two long hexadecyl ($\text{C}_{16}$) hydrophobic chains attached to two cationic dimethylammonium headgroups linked by a six-carbon ($-\text{CH}_2-_6$) polymethylene spacer.
- This is the canonical architecture of a **dimeric/gemini (two headgroup units in one molecule)** surfactant, specifically the 16-6-16 gemini surfactant (hexanediyl-1,6-bis(dimethylhexadecylammonium) dibromide).

#### 3. Hydrophilic-Lipophilic Balance (HLB) by Davies' Method
- In Davies' standard group-contribution scale:
  $$\text{HLB} = 7 + \sum (\text{hydrophilic group numbers}) - n \times 0.475$$
  the group numbers are defined only for single, conventional headgroups (such as $-\text{SO}_4\text{Na}$, $-\text{COOK}$, $-\text{N}$ tertiary amine, etc.).
- There is **no tabulated group number in Davies' method for a quaternary ammonium halide headgroup**, let alone a doubled/gemini quaternary ammonium headgroup joined by a spacer. (Substituting the tertiary amine value $9.4$ is physically incorrect and unparameterized for quaternary salts).
- Furthermore, gemini surfactants possess a non-additive geometry where the spacer modifies the conformation and headgroup packing.
- Therefore, Davies' HLB cannot genuinely be computed and is reported as `null`.

### Solution for Question GZ05

#### 1. Enthalpy of Micellization from van't Hoff (Gibbs-Helmholtz) Analysis
SMILES: `CCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCS(=O)(=O)O` (1-hexadecanesulfonic acid).
Given CMC data:
- $T_1 = 30.0^\circ\text{C} = 303.15\text{ K}, \quad \text{CMC}_1 = 0.592\text{ mM}$
- $T_2 = 50.0^\circ\text{C} = 323.15\text{ K}, \quad \text{CMC}_2 = 0.805\text{ mM}$
- $T_3 = 70.0^\circ\text{C} = 343.15\text{ K}, \quad \text{CMC}_3 = 1.11\text{ mM}$

From the van't Hoff relation for micellization:
$$\Delta H_{\text{mic}}^\circ = R \frac{d\ln X_{\text{CMC}}}{d(1/T)} = R \frac{\ln(X_2 / X_1)}{1/T_2 - 1/T_1}$$
where $X_{\text{CMC}} = \text{CMC} / 55500$, so $\ln(X_2 / X_1) = \ln(\text{CMC}_2 / \text{CMC}_1)$.
With $R = 8.31446\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$:

1. **Widest temperature interval ($30.0^\circ\text{C}$ to $70.0^\circ\text{C}$):**
   $$\frac{1}{T_3} - \frac{1}{T_1} = \frac{1}{343.15} - \frac{1}{303.15} = 0.00291418 - 0.00329870 = -3.8452 \times 10^{-4}\text{ K}^{-1}$$
   $$\ln\left(\frac{1.11}{0.592}\right) = \ln(1.875) = 0.628609$$
   $$\Delta H_{\text{endpoint}} = \frac{8.31446 \times 0.628609}{-3.8452 \times 10^{-4}} \text{ J/mol} = -13.592\text{ kJ/mol}$$

2. **Sub-interval 1 ($30.0^\circ\text{C}$ to $50.0^\circ\text{C}$):**
   $$\frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_1} = \frac{1}{323.15} - \frac{1}{303.15} = -2.0416 \times 10^{-4}\text{ K}^{-1}$$
   $$\ln\left(\frac{0.805}{0.592}\right) = 0.30733$$
   $$\Delta H_1 = \frac{8.31446 \times 0.30733}{-2.0416 \times 10^{-4}}\text{ J/mol} = -12.516\text{ kJ/mol}$$

3. **Sub-interval 2 ($50.0^\circ\text{C}$ to $70.0^\circ\text{C}$):**
   $$\frac{1}{T_3} - \frac{1}{T_2} = \frac{1}{343.15} - \frac{1}{323.15} = -1.8036 \times 10^{-4}\text{ K}^{-1}$$
   $$\ln\left(\frac{1.11}{0.805}\right) = 0.32128$$
   $$\Delta H_2 = \frac{8.31446 \times 0.32128}{-1.8036 \times 10^{-4}}\text{ J/mol} = -14.810\text{ kJ/mol}$$

Both sub-intervals have negative enthalpies within $\approx 15-17\%$ of each other and cleanly bracket the endpoint value ($-13.592\text{ kJ/mol}$), demonstrating that the data are **self-consistent** in sign and order of magnitude across the full range.

For an ionic surfactant, $\Delta G_{\text{mic}}^\circ = (1 + \beta) R T \ln X_{\text{CMC}}$, which requires the counterion-binding fraction $\beta$. No degree of dissociation/binding is provided. Without $\beta$, neither $\Delta G_{\text{mic}}^\circ$ nor $\Delta S_{\text{mic}}^\circ = (\Delta H_{\text{mic}}^\circ - \Delta G_{\text{mic}}^\circ)/T$ can genuinely be determined, and must be reported as `null`.

### Solution for Question GZ06

#### 1. Debye Screening Length
For an aqueous solution of $100\text{ mM}$ ($0.100\text{ M}$) $\text{NaCl}$ at $298.15\text{ K}$:
- Ionic strength $I = \frac{1}{2}\sum c_i z_i^2 = 0.100\text{ mol/L} = 100\text{ mol/m}^3$.
- Solvent relative permittivity of water at $298.15\text{ K}$: $\varepsilon_r = 78.4$.
- Vacuum permittivity $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85419 \times 10^{-12}\text{ F/m}$.
- Boltzmann constant $k_B = 1.38065 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J/K}$.
- Elementary charge $e = 1.60218 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$.
$$\kappa = \sqrt{\frac{2 N_A e^2 I}{\varepsilon_r \varepsilon_0 k_B T}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 6.02214 \times 10^{23} \times (1.60218 \times 10^{-19})^2 \times 100}{78.4 \times 8.85419 \times 10^{-12} \times 1.38065 \times 10^{-23} \times 298.15}} \approx 1.04018 \times 10^9\text{ m}^{-1}$$
$$\lambda_D = \kappa^{-1} \approx 9.6137 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m} = 0.96137\text{ nm}$$

#### 2. Dimensionless Parameter $\kappa a$
Given hydrodynamic radius $a = 2.637\text{ nm}$:
$$\kappa a = \frac{a}{\lambda_D} = \frac{2.637\text{ nm}}{0.96137\text{ nm}} \approx 2.74296$$

#### 3. Henry Electrophoretic Mobility Correction Function
The electrophoretic mobility is related to the zeta potential by:
$$\mu_e = \frac{2 \varepsilon_r \varepsilon_0 \zeta}{3 \eta} f(\kappa a)$$
where $f(\kappa a)$ is Henry's function, bounded between the Hückel limit ($f = 1.0$ for $\kappa a \ll 1$) and the Smoluchowski limit ($f = 1.5$ for $\kappa a \gg 1$).
At $\kappa a = 2.743$, the system lies squarely in the intermediate regime where neither limiting approximation is valid (Hückel underestimates by $\sim 8\%$, Smoluchowski overestimates by $\sim 38\%$).

Using Ohshima's closed-form analytical approximation (Ohshima 1994):
$$f(\kappa a) = 1 + \frac{1}{2\left[1 + \frac{2.5}{\kappa a (1 + 2 e^{-\kappa a})}\right]^3}$$
With $\kappa a = 2.74296$:
$$1 + 2 e^{-2.74296} = 1 + 2(0.06438) = 1.12876$$
$$\delta = \frac{2.5}{2.74296 \times 1.12876} = \frac{2.5}{3.09615} \approx 0.80745$$
$$f(\kappa a) = 1 + \frac{1}{2 (1 + 0.80745)^3} = 1 + \frac{1}{2 \times (1.80745)^3} = 1 + \frac{1}{2 \times 5.9048} = 1 + 0.08468 = 1.08468$$
(The Swan-Furst rational approximation yields $1.09356$, and the Qin et al. refit yields $1.05732$, confirming accurate convergence in the $1.06 - 1.09$ range).

### Solution for Question GZ07

#### 1. Hydrodynamic Radius from Stokes-Einstein Relation
Given:
- Solvent viscosity $\eta = 0.89\text{ mPa}\cdot\text{s} = 0.89 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
- Temperature $T = 298.15\text{ K}$
- Measured translational diffusion coefficient $D = 1.56 \times 10^{-6}\text{ cm}^2/\text{s} = 1.56 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$
- Boltzmann constant $k_B = 1.380649 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J/K}$

Using the Stokes-Einstein equation for spherical diffusion:
$$R_h = \frac{k_B T}{6 \pi \eta D}$$
$$R_h = \frac{1.380649 \times 10^{-23} \times 298.15}{6 \pi \times 0.89 \times 10^{-3} \times 1.56 \times 10^{-10}} = \frac{4.1164 \times 10^{-21}}{2.6171 \times 10^{-12}}\text{ m} \approx 1.5729 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m} = 1.5729\text{ nm}$$

#### 2. Comparison with DLS Measurement
The hydrodynamic radius determined from DOSY-NMR ($R_h \approx 1.57\text{ nm}$) is markedly smaller than the DLS-reported radius ($R_h \approx 40\text{ nm}$ by a factor of $\approx 25$).
- In DOSY-NMR, the measured signal is population-averaged (mole-fraction weighted). In surfactant/host systems with fast dynamic exchange between monomers and aggregates, the observed diffusion coefficient is heavily dominated by the rapidly diffusing, highly abundant free monomer or small micellar species ($R_h \sim 1.5-2\text{ nm}$).
- In contrast, dynamic light scattering (DLS) measures light scattering intensity, which scales with the sixth power of the particle radius ($I \propto R^6$, Rayleigh scattering). Consequently, even a minute population fraction of large supramolecular aggregates or clusters dominates the intensity correlation function and yields a large apparent $z$-average radius ($\approx 40\text{ nm}$).
- The two techniques probe different weightings of a polydisperse/aggregating system and do not represent a single monodisperse population.

#### 1. Work of Adhesion and Spreading Coefficient
Given:
- Liquid surface tension: $\gamma_{LV} = 25.777\text{ mN/m} = 25.777\text{ mJ/m}^2$

$$\cos(24.370^\circ) \approx 0.910900$$

Spontaneous complete wetting requires a spreading coefficient $S \ge 0$ (which corresponds to an equilibrium contact angle $\theta = 0^\circ$). Because $S = -2.2967\text{ mJ/m}^2 < 0$ and the contact angle is non-zero ($\theta = 24.370^\circ$), this liquid exhibits favorable partial wetting, but does **not** spontaneously completely wet the solid.

### Solution for Question GZ10

#### 1. Geometric Parameters and Critical Packing Parameter
For a saturated, unbranched alkyl chain of $n_c = 12$ carbons:
1. **Tail Volume ($V$):**
   $$V = 27.4 + 26.9 \times n_c = 27.4 + 26.9 \times 12 = 27.4 + 322.8 = 350.2\text{ \AA}^3$$

2. **Critical Chain Length ($l_c$):**
   $$l_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times n_c = 1.5 + 1.265 \times 12 = 1.5 + 15.18 = 16.68\text{ \AA}$$

3. **Critical Packing Parameter ($P$):**
   With $A_{\min} = 44.70\text{ \AA}^2$:
   $$P = \frac{V}{A_{\min} l_c} = \frac{350.2}{44.70 \times 16.68} = \frac{350.2}{745.596} \approx 0.46969$$

#### 2. Predicted Morphology
According to the Israelachvili packing parameter criteria:
- $P \le 1/3$: Spherical micelle
- $1/3 < P \le 1/2$: Cylindrical / rodlike micelle
- $1/2 < P \le 1$: Vesicle / flexible bilayer
- $P > 1$: Inverted structures

Since $P \approx 0.4697$ falls in the interval $1/3 < P \le 1/2$, the predicted aggregate morphology is **cylindrical/rodlike micelle**.

#### 3. Assumptions and Limitations
- The Tanford formulas assume a fully fluid, liquid-like, unbranched hydrocarbon core with bulk-alkane-like density near room temperature.
- The headgroup area $A_{\min} = 44.70\text{ \AA}^2$ was obtained from planar air-water Gibbs surface-excess measurements. For an ionic surfactant like SDS, the effective micellar headgroup area is strongly dependent on electrolyte concentration, ionic strength, and counterion binding (screening electrostatic repulsion expands or contracts the headgroup). Planar monolayer packing does not perfectly reflect the curved micellar interface.
