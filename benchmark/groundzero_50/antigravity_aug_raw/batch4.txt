### Chart Reading & Physical Reasoning (GZ-39_chart.png)
1. **Observation of Agreement and Divergence**:
   - Measured data (blue circles) and fitted pre-CMC adsorption isotherm (red curve) agree closely across low concentrations from $0.01\text{ mM}$ up to $\sim 1.6\text{ mM}$.
   - Above approximately **2–3 mM**, the measured data levels off into a constant surface tension plateau ($\sim 27.5–28\text{ mN/m}$), whereas the fitted model curve continues to decline monotonically towards $\sim 17.5\text{ mN/m}$ at $10\text{ mM}$.
2. **Physical Explanation**:
   - The fitted curve is a pre-CMC monolayer adsorption isotherm (e.g., Langmuir/Szyszkowski), which models the interface under the assumption that all surfactant in solution exists as monomeric molecules in equilibrium with the surface.
   - Once the critical micelle concentration (CMC) is reached at $\sim 2–3\text{ mM}$, additional surfactant molecules associate into bulk micelles rather than increasing monomer activity.
   - As a result, the chemical potential of monomeric surfactant remains virtually constant above the CMC, causing surface tension to plateau. Because the pre-CMC isotherm does not include the phase/pseudophase transition of micellization, it fails post-CMC and incorrectly predicts an ongoing drop in surface tension.
