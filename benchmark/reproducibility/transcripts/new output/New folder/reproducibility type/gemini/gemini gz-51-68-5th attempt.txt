To determine the maximum surface excess concentration ($\Gamma_{max}$) and the minimum area per molecule ($A_{min}$), we apply the Gibbs adsorption isotherm equation.

For an ionic surfactant in aqueous solution, the general form of the Gibbs equation is:
$$ \Gamma = -\frac{1}{nRT} \left( \frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C} \right) $$

The prefactor $n$ depends on the presence of background electrolyte. In pure water, an anionic surfactant (like $Na^+R^-$) requires $n=2$ because both the surfactant ion and its counterion must co-adsorb to maintain electroneutrality, meaning both contribute to the chemical potential gradient. However, in the presence of a *swamping* background electrolyte (0.1 M NaCl), the concentration of the $Na^+$ counterion in the bulk is effectively constant. The chemical potential of the counterion no longer changes significantly with surfactant addition, reducing the prefactor to **$n=1$**.

**1. Calculate Maximum Surface Excess ($\Gamma_{max}$)**
Given values:
*   Slope $\frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C} = -18.4 \text{ mN/m} = -0.0184 \text{ J/m}^2$
*   Gas constant $R = 8.3145 \text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$
*   Temperature $T = 298.15 \text{ K}$

$$ \Gamma_{max} = -\frac{1}{1 \times 8.3145 \times 298.15} \times (-0.0184) $$
$$ \Gamma_{max} = \frac{0.0184}{2478.97} \approx 7.422 \times 10^{-6} \text{ mol/m}^2 $$

**2. Calculate Minimum Area per Molecule ($A_{min}$)**
