$$ \Delta G_{mic} = RT \ln(CMC) $$

### 2. The Gibbs-Helmholtz Relationship
The real van't Hoff (Gibbs-Helmholtz) relationship defines how the ratio of $\Delta G$ to temperature changes with respect to temperature:
$$ \left( \frac{\partial (\Delta G_{mic} / T)}{\partial T} \right)_P = -\frac{\Delta H_{mic}}{T^2} $$

$$ R \frac{d \ln(CMC)}{dT} = -\frac{\Delta H_{mic}}{T^2} $$

Rearranging to integrate with respect to $1/T$:
$$ d \ln(CMC) = \frac{\Delta H_{mic}}{R} d\left(\frac{1}{T}\right) $$

Assuming $\Delta H_{mic}$ remains constant over this small temperature range, integrating from $T_1$ to $T_2$ yields:

To determine the maximum surface excess concentration and minimum area per molecule, we apply the Gibbs adsorption isotherm.

**1. The Gibbs Adsorption Isotherm**
The surface excess concentration ($\Gamma$) is determined from the change in surface tension ($\gamma$) with respect to the logarithm of surfactant concentration ($C$):
$$ \Gamma_{max} = -\frac{1}{nRT} \left( \frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C} \right) $$

**2. Determining the Prefactor ($n$)**
For an ionic surfactant in pure water, the prefactor $n=2$ because both the surfactant ion and its counterion must co-adsorb to maintain electroneutrality, and both species' concentrations change simultaneously. However, the given system contains $0.1 \text{ M}$ NaCl as a background electrolyte. 

Because the background electrolyte concentration is typically in massive excess compared to the surfactant monomer concentration (which is below the CMC), the chemical potential of the counterion is effectively constant and decoupled from the surfactant concentration. Therefore, the counterion does not contribute to the thermodynamic driving force for adsorption as the surfactant concentration varies. Thus, in the presence of a swamping background electrolyte, $n = 1$.

**3. Calculating Maximum Surface Excess ($\Gamma_{max}$)**
Given values:
*   $n = 1$
*   $R = 8.314 \text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$
*   $T = 298.15 \text{ K}$
*   $\frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C} = -18.4 \text{ mN/m} = -18.4 \times 10^{-3} \text{ N/m}$ (or $\text{J/m}^2$)

$$ \Gamma_{max} = -\frac{1}{(1)(8.314)(298.15)} (-18.4 \times 10^{-3}) $$
