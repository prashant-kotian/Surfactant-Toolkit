GZ-61  — Zeta potential of an anionic micelle
================================================================================

Given:
  Hydrodynamic radius a = 2.5 nm
  1:1 background electrolyte, ionic strength I = 20 mM
  T = 298.15 K, relative permittivity of water = 78.4
  Electrophoretic mobility = 2.7 um*cm/(V*s) = 2.7e-8 m^2/(V*s)
  Solvent viscosity = 0.8903 mPa*s

Step 1 — Debye parameter and kappa*a.
  For a 1:1 electrolyte, kappa^2 = 2 F^2 I / (er e0 R T), with I = 20 mol/m^3.
  This gives 1/kappa = 2.15 nm, so
      kappa*a = 2.5 / 2.15 = 1.16
  This is the intermediate regime — neither the Huckel limit (kappa*a << 1)
  nor the Smoluchowski limit (kappa*a >> 1) applies, so the full Henry
  equation is required:
      mu = (2 er e0 zeta) / (3 eta) * f(kappa*a)

Step 2 — Henry's function.
  Ohshima's closed-form approximation gives f(kappa*a = 1.16) = 1.040
  (close to the Huckel value of 1, as expected for a small particle in a
  moderately dilute electrolyte).

Step 3 — Solve for zeta.
      zeta = 3 eta mu / (2 er e0 f) = 50.0 mV in magnitude.
  Since the micelle is anionic, the zeta potential is negative: zeta = -50 mV.

  (Reference: Smoluchowski alone would give ~35 mV, Huckel ~52 mV; Henry with
  kappa*a ~ 1.16 correctly sits near the Huckel end.)

FINAL_JSON:
{
  "kappa_a": 1.163,
  "zeta_potential_mV": -50.0
}

GZ-62  — CMC and counterion binding degree from conductivity
================================================================================

Method: conductivity (Williams / Frahm) method. Specific conductivity vs
concentration is bilinear — steep below the CMC (monomers as free ions),
shallower above (micelles carry charge less efficiently and bind counterions).

Fitting the two linear regimes (both R^2 = 0.9999):
  Pre-micellar (1-5 mM):    slope S1 = 0.6414 mS/cm per mM
  Post-micellar (6.5-12 mM): slope S2 = 0.2956 mS/cm per mM

CMC = intersection of the two lines = 5.97 mM

Counterion binding degree (conductivity method):
  beta = 1 - S2/S1 = 1 - 0.2956/0.6414 = 0.54
  (~54% of counterions are bound to the micelle surface.)

FINAL_JSON:
{
  "cmc_mM": 5.97,
  "counterion_binding_degree": 0.539
}

GZ-63  — Micelle aggregation number from fluorescence quenching
================================================================================

Given:
  Total surfactant Ct = 50 mM, cmc = 8 mM  ->  micellized surfactant = 42 mM
  Model (Turro-Yekta): ln(I0/I) = N[Q]/(Ct - cmc), straight line through origin
  So slope of ln(I0/I) vs [Q] = N/(Ct - cmc).

Point-by-point ln(I0/I) and ratio to [Q]:
  [Q]=0.5  ln(I0/I)=0.7144  ratio=1.4287
  [Q]=1.0  ln(I0/I)=1.4284  ratio=1.4284
  [Q]=1.5  ln(I0/I)=2.1430  ratio=1.4287
  [Q]=2.0  ln(I0/I)=2.8577  ratio=1.4289
  [Q]=2.5  ln(I0/I)=3.2139  ratio=1.2856   <- deviates (curvature)
  [Q]=3.0  ln(I0/I)=3.5132  ratio=1.1711   <- deviates (curvature)

The first four points fall on a perfect line through the origin (slope =
1.4287 mM^-1); the last two curve downward (simple Poisson model breaks down at
high quencher occupancy) and are excluded.

  N = slope x (Ct - cmc) = 1.4287 x 42 = 60.0

GZ-64  — Best-estimate CMC for CTAB in pure water
================================================================================

Three reported values:
  0.876 mM  (single-lab conductometric measurement)
  0.904 mM  (literature average across 15 independent sources)
  0.930 mM  (streaming-potential measurement)

Reasoning: the values are not equal in weight. The 0.876 and 0.930 mM figures
are each one determination by one method and bracket the middle value nearly
symmetrically. The 0.904 mM value is a meta-average of 15 independent sources,
so it embeds far more measurements and averages out method/lab systematics — it
deserves the dominant weight.

The two natural estimators converge:
  Literature consensus alone:            0.904 mM
  Unweighted mean of all three:  (0.876+0.904+0.930)/3 = 0.903 mM

They agree to within 0.001 mM, and the single-lab values sit symmetrically
(-0.028 and +0.026 mM) about the consensus. Result rounds to ~0.90 mM,
matching the accepted textbook CMC for CTAB in pure water (~0.9-1.0 mM).

FINAL_JSON:
{
  "best_estimate_cmc_mM": 0.904,
  "resolution_method": "Weighted judgment favoring the 0.904 mM value because it is itself an average of 15 independent sources (far more robust than either single-lab measurement), which conveniently coincides with the unweighted mean of all three values (0.903 mM). The two single measurements (0.876 conductometric, 0.930 streaming-potential) bracket it nearly symmetrically, confirming no bias correction is needed. Result rounds to ~0.90 mM, consistent with the accepted literature CMC for CTAB in pure water at room temperature."
}

GZ-65  — Gamma_max and A_min from Gibbs adsorption isotherm
================================================================================

Given:
  Anionic surfactant, 0.1 M NaCl background electrolyte, T = 298.15 K
  Slope dgamma/d ln(C) in premicellar region = -18.4 mN/m

Gibbs adsorption isotherm:  Gamma_max = -(1/nRT) * (dgamma/d lnC)

Key point — the prefactor n. Although the surfactant is ionic (which would
normally need n = 2), the solution has a large excess of background electrolyte
(0.1 M NaCl >> pre-CMC surfactant). The swamping salt fixes the counterion
activity, so the counterion term drops out and n = 1 applies (the ionic
surfactant behaves like a nonionic one).

  Gamma_max = 18.4e-3 / (8.314 x 298.15) = 7.42e-6 mol/m^2

  A_min = 1 / (N_A x Gamma_max)
        = 1 / (6.022e23 x 7.42e-6)
        = 2.24e-19 m^2 = 0.224 nm^2

FINAL_JSON:
{
  "gamma_max_mol_per_m2": 7.42e-06,
  "a_min_nm2": 0.224
}
