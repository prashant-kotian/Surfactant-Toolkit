# GZ-66 — Critical Packing Parameter and Davies HLB
################################################################################

CRITICAL PACKING PARAMETER (Tanford relations, n_c = 12)

The dodecyl tail has 12 carbons, so:
  - Tail volume:          V   = 27.4 + 26.9(12) = 27.4 + 322.8 = 350.2 A^3
  - Critical chain length: l_c = 1.5 + 1.265(12) = 1.5 + 15.18 = 16.68 A
  - Headgroup area:        a0  = 49.37 A^2

  CPP = V / (a0 * l_c) = 350.2 / (49.37 x 16.68) = 350.2 / 823.49 = 0.425

Since 1/3 (0.333) < 0.425 < 1/2 (0.500), the packing parameter falls in the
range associated with CYLINDRICAL (rod-like) micelles.


HLB (Davies group-contribution method)

  - Hydrophilic contribution: one -OSO3Na = 38.6
  - Lipophilic contribution:  (11 x -CH2-) + (1 x -CH3) = 12 x 0.475 = 5.7

  HLB = 7 + 38.6 - 5.7 = 39.9


CONSISTENCY OF THE TWO DESCRIPTORS

The two descriptors agree on the coarse, qualitative picture but diverge on the
fine detail.

Where they agree: both point unambiguously to a hydrophilic, water-soluble
surfactant that forms *normal* (oil-in-water, convex-toward-oil) aggregates
rather than inverted or lamellar phases. A CPP well below 1 rules out planar
bilayers and inverted structures, and an HLB far above ~13-15 signals a strong
detergent/solubilizer that stabilizes O/W systems.

Where they are in tension: the two scales encode curvature in opposite
directions, and quantitatively they don't line up. HLB rises with
hydrophilicity, and CPP falls with hydrophilicity (a large, strongly
hydrated/charged headgroup means large a0, hence small CPP and high interfacial
curvature). An HLB of ~40 is extreme -- it sits far above the nominal 0-20
Griffin scale, a known artifact of Davies' large ionic group numbers -- and
taken at face value it implies the *maximum* curvature case, i.e. small
spherical micelles (CPP < 1/3). The computed CPP of 0.425, however, lands in the
cylindrical-micelle window, i.e. intermediate curvature. So the packing
parameter is somewhat *higher* (lower curvature) than the very large HLB would
lead you to expect.

The source of the mismatch is instructive rather than alarming. The CPP is quite
sensitive to a0, and 49.37 A^2 is on the compact side for an ionic headgroup --
electrostatic repulsion between sulfate heads typically pushes the effective
area toward 55-65 A^2 under conditions of significant screening, which would drop
the CPP back below 1/3 and into the spherical regime that matches SDS's
well-known low-concentration behavior. Meanwhile Davies' method is purely
additive and structural: it counts the ionic group's chemical hydrophilicity but
is blind to ionic strength, counterion binding, and the geometric packing
constraints that the CPP captures. In short, the descriptors are consistent in
sign and in the normal-micelle conclusion, but only partially consistent in the
predicted degree of curvature.

# GZ-67 — Hydrophilic-Lipophilic Difference (HLD) and Winsor Type
################################################################################

Inputs: characteristic curvature Cc = -2.63; EACN-scaling constant k = 0.16
(anionic); toluene EACN = 0; salinity S = 5; alpha = 0.01/K; deltaT = 0.

HLD CALCULATION

  HLD = ln(S) - k*EACN - alpha*deltaT + Cc
      = ln(5) - 0.16*0 - 0.01*0 + (-2.63)
      = 1.609 - 0 - 0 - 2.63
      = -1.02

Since HLD < 0, the surfactant is net hydrophilic: it forms a normal
oil-in-water microemulsion in equilibrium with an excess oil (toluene) phase.
This is WINSOR TYPE I.


DOES beta = 0.728 SUPPORT THE TYPE I PREDICTION?

Short answer: yes at the qualitative and mechanistic level, but beta is a
supporting indicator rather than a determinant of Winsor type.

Qualitatively consistent. A counterion binding degree of 0.728 is a property
measured on well-defined aqueous micelles -- normal, curved-toward-oil
aggregates dispersed in the water phase. The existence of such micelles with a
stable, highly charged Stern layer is the microscopic signature of a hydrophilic
ionic surfactant that resides on the water side of the phase diagram -- exactly
the population HLD places in Winsor I (o/w).

Mechanistically linked (the stronger form of support). Counterion binding is the
physical mechanism behind the salinity term in the HLD equation. Adding salt
(raising ln S) drives an anionic surfactant Type I -> III -> II precisely by
screening the sulfate headgroup charge and increasing counterion association at
the interface; this shrinks the effective headgroup area, reduces spontaneous
curvature toward oil, and pushes HLD toward zero. A high beta = 0.728 is the
interfacial-charge measurement that this same screening is operative. So beta and
the ln(S) contribution are two experimental windows onto one phenomenon --
surface-charge neutralization.

The caveat. beta does not by itself fix the Winsor type: one can measure high
beta and still be Type I, III, or II depending on total salinity, because beta
modulates hydrophilicity but the strongly negative intrinsic Cc (-2.63) still
dominates the balance. If anything, extensive counterion binding works *against*
deep hydrophilicity and nudges the system toward the Type III transition. That
fits the numbers: HLD = -1.02 is only moderately negative, i.e. Type I but not
far from the Type III optimum (HLD = 0). A modest further increase in salinity
(ln S rising by ~1, i.e. S up ~2.8x) would reach HLD ~ 0, and the microscopic
driver would be exactly the enhanced counterion binding that beta = 0.728 already
reports.

# GZ-68 — Packing, Ellipsoidal Shape, and Hydrodynamic Radius

CRITICAL PACKING PARAMETER

With n_c = 12: V = 27.4 + 26.9(12) = 350.2 A^3 and l_c = 1.5 + 1.265(12) = 16.68 A.

  CPP = V / (a0 * l_c) = 350.2 / (49.37 x 16.68) = 0.425

This places SDS in the cylindrical-micelle window (1/3 < CPP < 1/2) by the strict
criterion, though it sits near the spherical boundary.


AXIAL RATIO OF THE PROLATE CORE

Core volume = N*V = 63 x 350.2 = 22,062.6 A^3.
Pinning the minor semi-axis at b = l_c = 16.68 A and conserving volume with
(4/3)*pi*a*b^2 = 22,062.6 A^3 gives the major semi-axis a = 18.93 A.

  Axial ratio a/b = 18.93 / 16.68 = 1.135

The micelle is only very mildly prolate -- barely deformed from a sphere.


HYDRODYNAMIC RADIUS: NAIVE vs. PERRIN-CORRECTED

Stokes-Einstein radius from D = 0.093e-5 cm^2/s (eta = 0.8903 mPa*s, 298.15 K):

  R_h = k_B*T / (6*pi*eta*D) = 2.638 nm   (uncorrected, sphere-assumed)

Perrin translational friction factor for a prolate spheroid of axial ratio
p = 1.135, relative to an equal-volume sphere:

  F = sqrt(p^2 - 1) / [ p^(1/3) * ln(p + sqrt(p^2 - 1)) ] = 1.0014

Because friction rises above the sphere value by only 0.14%, the shape-corrected
(equal-volume-sphere) radius is

  R_h,true = R_h,naive / F = 2.638 / 1.0014 = 2.634 nm

The correction is negligible here -- about 4 pm, well inside experimental
uncertainty -- precisely because the aggregation number (63) and the geometry
produce an axial ratio of only 1.135. Shape corrections to hydrodynamic radii
matter only for genuinely elongated aggregates (p >~ 2); a near-spherical SDS
micelle is well described by the naive Stokes-Einstein value. Note the internal
tension the numbers expose: the CPP of 0.425 formally predicts cylindrical
growth, yet the measured aggregation number forces a nearly spherical core -- a
reminder that a0 = 49.37 A^2 is on the compact side for a charged sulfate head
and that the strict CPP cutoffs are guidelines, not sharp phase boundaries.
