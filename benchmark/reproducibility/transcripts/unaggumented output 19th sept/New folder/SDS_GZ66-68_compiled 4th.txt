IS beta = 0.728 CONSISTENT WITH THE WINSOR I PREDICTION?
-------------------------------------------------------
Yes, and it offers genuine independent molecular-level support, provided one
reads it carefully.

Both quantities are ultimately governed by the electrostatic state of the
sulfate headgroup layer, but they are measured on completely different footings:
HLD is a thermodynamic phase-behavior descriptor built from the characteristic
curvature Cc and the salinity/temperature/oil terms, while beta comes from a
separate conductivity- or EPR-type experiment counting the fraction of Na+
condensed onto the micelle surface. Their agreement is therefore not circular.

The key reading is the residual, not the bound, charge. A binding degree of
0.728 leaves 1 - beta ~ 0.27 of the counterions dissociated, so the micelle
surface retains substantial net negative charge. That residual charge sustains
headgroup-headgroup repulsion, keeps the effective head area large, and
maintains the high positive spontaneous (interfacial) curvature that defines
normal micelles — exactly the molecular picture behind a strongly negative Cc
(-2.63) and an HLD comfortably below zero. So the surfactant remaining net
hydrophilic (Winsor I) is consistent with a micelle that is highly, but not
fully, charge-neutralized.

There is a second, subtler layer of support. Adding salt to an ionic surfactant
does two coupled things: in the HLD equation it raises the ln(S) term, driving
HLD upward toward the Winsor I -> III -> II sequence, and physically it screens
the interface and pushes more counterions onto the surface, i.e. it raises beta.
A beta of 0.728 at 5% NaCl-equivalent salinity is on the high side for SDS
(near-neutral binding of ~0.6-0.7 in low salt), which is the molecular signature
of exactly the screening that the ln(S) term is accounting for. The system is
being pushed toward the optimum-formulation transition — but HLD = -1.02 is
still clearly negative, not marginal, so it has not crossed over. The
high-but-not-complete counterion binding mirrors this precisely: strong
screening, yet enough residual charge to keep the film hydrophilic and the phase
behavior in Winsor I.

The one caveat worth stating is directional consistency versus quantitative
coupling. A higher beta, taken alone, reduces headgroup repulsion and would tend
to make Cc less negative and nudge HLD upward — so beta and hydrophilicity are
inversely related at the margin. The two descriptors are consistent here not
because "high beta means more hydrophilic," but because even this elevated beta
leaves the interface charged enough to sit on the Winsor I side of the
transition. Both independent measurements therefore converge on the same
conclusion: a hydrophilic-dominated, normal-micelle-forming surfactant that is
salt-sensitive and trending toward — but not yet at — its optimum formulation.
