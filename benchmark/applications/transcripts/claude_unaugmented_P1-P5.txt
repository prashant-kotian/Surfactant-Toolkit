QUESTION 1 — CMC OF A DIACETYLATED ACIDIC SOPHOROLIPID
================================================================================

Target: PubChem CID 11856871
SMILES: CC(CCCCCC/C=C/CCCCCCCC(=O)O)O[C@H]1[C@@H]([C@H]([C@@H]([C@H](O1)COC(=O)C)O)O)O[C@H]2[C@@H]([C@H]([C@@H]([C@H](O2)COC(=O)C)O)O)O
Request: Derived/computed CMC prediction in water at 25 C (not a looked-up value).

--- BOTTOM LINE ---
CMC ~ 0.1-0.4 mM at 25 C; central estimate ~0.2 mM (~140 mg/L).

--- STEP 1: STRUCTURE & MW ---
An 18-carbon acyl chain (9-octadecenoic acid; one C=C, written E here, natural
SLs are usually Z) glycosidically linked at the SUBTERMINAL C17 (omega-1) carbon
to sophorose (two beta-glucopyranoses, 1->2), with BOTH primary 6-OH groups
acetylated. => diacetylated ACIDIC (open-chain, free-COOH) sophorolipid.

Hand-counted formula: C34H58O15, MW ~ 706.8 g/mol
(6 degrees of unsaturation: COOH + 2 acetyl C=O + 1 alkene + 2 rings;
 H = 2*34 + 2 - 2*6 = 58). Matches expected mass for CID 11856871.

--- STEP 2: KEY INSIGHT — EFFECTIVE TAIL LENGTH, NOT CARBON COUNT ---
A naive "C18 tail" would predict too low a CMC. Three features shorten the
effective hydrophobe:
  1. Subterminal attachment: sugar on C17 leaves only a 1-C methyl stub on one
     side; headgroup sits near the chain end, not at the terminus.
  2. Free terminal COOH at C1: polar end pulls back to water -> chain loops ->
     roughly halves effective penetration depth.
  3. Mid-chain C=C: costs ~1-1.5 CH2-equivalents of hydrophobicity.
Net effective chain n_eff ~ 11-13 CH2. Two acetyls make the head LESS
hydrophilic -> nudges CMC toward the lower end.

--- STEP 3: TWO INDEPENDENT ESTIMATES ---
(a) Klevens homolog fit, calibrated on alkyl-beta-glucosides
    (C8->20 mM, C10->2.2 mM, C12->0.19 mM):
    log10(CMC) = 2.35 - 0.506 * n_eff
       n_eff=11 -> 0.61 mM (430 mg/L)
       n_eff=12 -> 0.19 mM (134 mg/L)
       n_eff=13 -> 0.059 mM (42 mg/L)

(b) Transfer-free-energy model
    dG_mic = n_buried*(-3.0 kJ/mol) + 6 kJ/mol (head/curvature/COOH penalty);
    CMC = 55.5 * exp(dG/RT):
       buried CH2=11 -> 1.0 mM
       buried CH2=12 -> 0.31 mM
       buried CH2=13 -> 0.092 mM

--- STEP 4: RECONCILE ---
Both routes cross at 0.1-0.4 mM for n_eff ~ 12. Weight toward ~0.2 mM because
diacetylation lowers headgroup hydrophilicity.
PREDICTION: CMC ~ 0.2 mM (~1.4e-4 M, ~140 mg/L) at 25 C; band 0.06-0.6 mM.

--- ASSUMPTIONS / CAVEATS ---
- Treated as effectively NONIONIC (COOH pKa ~5, mostly protonated in pure water;
  adding salt or raising pH would RAISE the CMC).
- Calibration borrowed from alkyl-glucosides; real sophorose+2 acetyls head
  differs (~ +/-0.3 log units on the intercept).
- Took SMILES as given (E-alkene, acidic/open form). The LACTONIC form packs
  tighter -> lower CMC (~tens of uM); different molecule.
- CMC is method- and purity-dependent (factor ~2-3 spread).

QUESTION 2 — SURFACE CHARGE STATE OF DI-RHAMNOLIPID (RhaRhaC10C10)
================================================================================

Target: PubChem CID 5458394
SMILES: CCCCCCCC(CC(=O)O)OC(=O)CC(CCCCCCC)O[C@H]1[C@@H]([C@@H]([C@H]([C@@H](O1)C)O)O)O[C@H]2[C@@H]([C@@H]([C@H]([C@@H](O2)C)O)O)O
System: 25 mg/mL, no deliberately added electrolyte.
Request: Real degree of ionization (alpha) and surface potential (psi0) at
pH 5.0 and pH 7.5, accounting for electrostatic self-limiting effects at the
aggregate surface (not just bare solution pKa).

--- SETUP ---
Molecule: Rha-Rha-C10-C10, C32H58O13, MW 650.8 g/mol. One titratable group
(the beta-ester fatty-acid COOH). 25 mg/mL = 38.4 mM (~200x CMC), so essentially
all aggregated.

This is a CHARGE-REGULATION problem: as carboxyls ionize, the surface goes
negative, local [H+] rises (Boltzmann), suppressing further ionization. alpha
and psi0 must be solved together.

Coupled equations (solved self-consistently):
  1. Charge regulation: alpha/(1-alpha) = 10^(pH - pKa_int) * exp(e*psi0/kT)
  2. Surface charge:     sigma = e*alpha / a
  3. Gouy-Chapman (1:1, planar): sigma = sqrt(8*c*eps*eps0*kT)*sinh(e|psi0|/2kT)
  4. Salt-free closure:  c = alpha * C_surf  (only small ions = released
                         counterions / base used to set pH)

Assumptions: pKa_int = 5.0 (intrinsic, charge-free), area/head a = 60 A^2,
T = 25 C, eps = 78.4.

--- RESULTS ---
                              pH 5.0           pH 7.5
  Degree of ionization alpha  ~ 0.04           ~ 0.53
  Surface potential psi0      ~ -80 mV         ~ -145 mV
  (naive HH would give)       0.50             0.997
  effective counterion str.   ~1.6 mM          ~20 mM
  apparent (titration) pKa    ~6.4             ~7.5

Interpretation:
- pH 5.0: bare HH says half-ionized; reality holds only ~4% ionized. The
  electrostatic penalty raises apparent pKa to ~6.4 (shift ~ +1.4 units).
  Surface is nearly neutral, fatty-acid-like.
- pH 7.5: HH says 99.7% charged; reality self-limits at ~53%. psi0 ~ -145 mV
  stalls deprotonation near half-ionization (apparent pKa dragged up to ~pH).

Robustness (pKa_int 4.8-5.5, a 0.55-0.70 nm^2):
  pH 5.0 stays alpha ~ 0.02-0.06 (psi0 -64 to -89 mV)
  pH 7.5 stays alpha ~ 0.30-0.66 (psi0 -130 to -152 mV)
Planar GC slightly overstates |psi0| vs a curved micelle; higher real ionic
strength would raise alpha somewhat. Qualitative picture is stable.

--- STRUCTURAL IMPLICATIONS (packing parameter p = v/(a_eff*lc)) ---
Two C10 chains: v ~ 540 A^3, lc ~ 1.3 nm. Lever is a_eff, swollen by head-head
repulsion as alpha rises.
  pH 5.0 (alpha~0.04, tight heads a_eff~0.6 nm^2): p ~ 0.6-0.7
          -> low curvature: bilayers / vesicles / lamellar
  pH 7.5 (alpha~0.5, swollen heads a_eff~0.9 nm^2): p ~ 0.45
          -> high curvature: small spherical/ellipsoidal micelles

EXPECTED: pH-driven vesicle/lamellar (acidic) -> micelle (neutral/alkaline)
transition between ~5 and ~7.5 — classic pH-responsive di-rhamnolipid behavior,
driven by the electrostatically self-regulated surface charge, not the bulk pKa.

QUESTION 3 — MIXED SYSTEM: SDS + DDAO
================================================================================

Surfactant A: sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), anionic
Surfactant B: N,N-dimethyldodecylamine N-oxide (DDAO), semipolar/nonionic at
              neutral pH
Request: Micellar composition and interaction parameter across composition,
via more than one approach; assess which to trust.

--- DATA vs DERIVED (honesty flag) ---
Hard data (25 C, no added salt): CMC_SDS = 8.2 mM; CMC_DDAO = 2.1 mM (neutral).
Could not extract a raw experimental mixed-CMC(alpha) table (sources paywalled),
so the mixed-CMC curve was generated by forward regular-solution theory anchored
to the literature equimolar value beta ~ -3.3, then processed by the extraction
methods. The flat beta = -3.3 is partly an INPUT; the informative outputs are the
synergy magnitude, the composition (two methods agree), and method behavior.

--- RESULTS (alpha = overall SDS mole fraction; CMC in mM) ---
 alpha  C_ideal  C_mixed  synergy  x1(Rub)  beta(Rub)  x1(Moto)
  0.1    2.27     1.70     25%     0.185    -3.3       0.185
  0.3    2.70     1.58     42%     0.296    -3.3       0.298
  0.5    3.34     1.67     50%     0.373    -3.3       0.368
  0.7    4.38     1.96     55%     0.451    -3.3       0.441
  0.9    6.35     2.93     54%     0.579    -3.3       0.592

Methods:
- Clint (ideal mixing): baseline, no interaction. Real CMC sits 25-56% below it;
  that gap is the synergy.
- Rubingh / regular-solution theory: x1 and beta from activity coefficients
  f1=exp[beta(1-x1)^2], f2=exp[beta x1^2].
- Motomura (model-free lever): x1 = alpha - alpha(1-alpha)(1/C)(dC/dalpha).

Physical readout:
  1. Genuine synergism: mixed CMC dips below even the lower pure CMC
     (min ~1.58 mM near alpha=0.3 vs 2.1 mM pure DDAO).
     |beta|=3.3 > |ln(C1/C2)|=1.36 -> criterion met.
  2. Micelle is DDAO-RICH almost everywhere: at alpha=0.5 micelle is ~37% SDS /
     63% DDAO. Need alpha > ~0.8 before micelle reaches 50% SDS.
  3. Rubingh and Motomura agree on x1 to +/-0.01 -> composition is robust.

Asymmetric-synergy check: feeding a composition-dependent beta (more negative on
DDAO-rich side) makes Rubingh's pointwise beta run from ~-3.8 (alpha=0.1) to
~-3.2 (alpha=0.9). A single-beta fit would hide that.

--- WHICH APPROACH I TRUST ---
- Pure CMCs: trust as data.
- Composition (x1): trust — Rubingh and Motomura converge (DDAO-rich micelle).
  Motomura is more principled (no RST assumption) but needs a derivative of the
  CMC curve, so noisy near endpoints; quote Rubingh x1, use Motomura as check.
- Interaction parameter beta: report ~ -3 to -4 at neutral pH as an EFFECTIVE
  value, with caveats:
    (i)  ionic/nonionic counterion ambiguity (ν=2 correction shifts beta ~1 unit)
    (ii) beta is composition-dependent (asymmetric synergy) — single beta is an
         approximation (regular-solution assumes zero excess entropy, one beta).
- DECISIVE CAVEAT = pH. Treated DDAO as neutral (valid pH >~ 7). If DDAO is
  protonated (apparent pKa ~4-5, shifts up in SDS's field) the system becomes
  catanionic and beta plunges to ~ -10 to -16, with precipitation/vesicle risk
  near equimolar. Pin down pH before trusting any number.

Net: trust composition (DDAO-rich), treat beta ~ -3.3 as a neutral-pH effective
value good to ~ +/-1 unit, treat pH as the dominant uncontrolled variable.

Sources:

  PMC: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11008254/)

  SMILES: CCCCCCCCCCCC(=O)NCCC[N+](C)(C)CC.[Br-]
  = C12 lauramidopropyl dimethyl-ethyl ammonium bromide (a quat).
Surfactant B ("SLG"), ANIONIC: monosodium N-lauroyl glutamate
  PubChem CID 23668603
  SMILES: CCCCCCCCCCCC(=O)N[C@@H](CCC(=O)O)C(=O)[O-].[Na+]
  = one COO-Na+, one still-protonated COOH.
=> CATANIONIC pair: direct cation-anion pairing, NOT ion-dipole.
Request: Micellar composition and interaction parameter across composition,
via more than one approach; assess which to trust.

--- DATA vs DERIVED (honesty flag) ---
Pure CMCs are ESTIMATES:
- SLG ~ 5 mM. (Reference lists sodium COCOYL glutamate 0.4-0.5 mM, but cocoyl is
  a longer-average-chain mixture; pure C12 monosodium lauroyl glutamate is higher,

- 12-EtBr ~ 8 mM. (From DTAB ~15 mM, lowered by amidopropyl spacer + ethyl group.)
Relevant SLG+cationic-quat study is paywalled; anchored beta ~ -12 (cation/anion
pairs run -10 to -25). Treat specific numbers as order-of-magnitude.

Sensitivity of equimolar CMC to beta (ideal = 6.15 mM):
  beta=-8  -> 0.85 mM;  beta=-12 -> 0.31 mM;
  beta=-16 -> 0.12 mM;  beta=-20 -> 0.043 mM.

Catanionic signatures (all present):
  1. Order-of-magnitude CMC collapse: mixed CMC 11-20x below ideal and far below
     either pure surfactant (~0.3 mM vs 5-8 mM). (SDS/DDAO only ~2x.)
  2. Micelle composition LOCKS near 1:1: x1 ~ 0.40->0.56 across all bulk alpha.
     Cation and anion enter as a pair. Rubingh and Motomura agree +/-0.01.
  3. Driver = counterion release: pairing frees Na+ and Br- (~ free NaBr); that
     entropy + headgroup electrostatics gives |beta| >> |ln(C1/C2)| = 0.47.

--- WHERE RST BREAKS (worst exactly where synergy is strongest) ---
The 1:1 ion pair is effectively a double-tailed, near-neutral amphiphile. Near
alpha ~ 0.5 such systems typically PRECIPITATE / coacervate / form vesicles or
lamellar phases rather than simple micelles -> a clean mixed-micelle CMC may not
exist, and the pseudo-phase/RST framework has no valid object there. The smooth
beta=-12 curve is most suspect at equimolar.
Molecule-specific wrinkles:
- SLG's second carboxyl: monosodium form has one protonated COOH. Raise pH ->
  ionizes (-1 -> -2) -> stronger pairing, deeper synergy, wider precipitation
  window; lower pH -> weaker. pH is the master variable.
- Amide H-bonding (both tails) + soft polarizable Br- add attraction beyond pure
  electrostatics -> beta more negative than a sulfate/trimethyl analog.

--- WHICH APPROACH I TRUST ---
- Composition (x1 ~ 1/2): trust. Rubingh and Motomura converge; matches 1:1
  pairing expectation. Quote Rubingh x1, Motomura as cross-check.
- Clint: not reality here, only the yardstick that makes the ~20x synergy visible.
- Single Rubingh beta: report as EFFECTIVE -10 to -15 on the measurable
  (micelle-forming) branches only; do NOT trust as a true pair-interaction energy
  because (i) ionic/ionic counterion treatment unresolved (~1-2 unit ambiguity),
  (ii) beta absorbs counterion-release entropy, (iii) RST assumes one beta + a
  micellar phase, both fail near equimolar.
- For a quantitative beta: use a molecular-thermodynamic catanionic model
  (Blankschtein/Shiloach or Nagarajan type) with explicit electrostatic +
  counterion-release terms, fit to measured mixed CMCs on the micellar branches,
  plus a phase map (turbidity/DLS) for the precipitation/vesicle window.

Bottom line: expect a near-equimolar mixed micelle, a ~20x (or larger) CMC
depression, and a precipitation/vesicle regime around 1:1 rather than a clean
micellar CMC. Composition is robust across methods; the interaction NUMBER is an
effective, pH- and counterion-contingent quantity to quote only with caveats.

METHODS NOTE (common to Q3 & Q4)
================================================================================
Clint (ideal):      1/C_ideal = alpha/C1 + (1-alpha)/C2
Rubingh (RST):      solve x1 from
   x1^2 * ln(alpha*C_M/(x1*C1)) = (1-x1)^2 * ln((1-alpha)*C_M/((1-x1)*C2))
   then beta = ln(alpha*C_M/(x1*C1)) / (1-x1)^2
Motomura (lever):   x1 = alpha - alpha*(1-alpha)/C_M * dC_M/dalpha
Synergism criterion (mixed-CMC minimum): beta < 0 and |beta| > |ln(C1/C2)|
