where \(S^*\) is in g NaCl/100 mL, \(E\) is EACN, \(T\) is °C, and \(C_A\) is the alcohol mass fraction in the surfactant-plus-alcohol pseudocomponent. The reported RMSE is 0.038 in \(\ln S^*\). I derived

## Which numbers are most credible?

For **free monomer and total micellization near and above the CMC**, I trust the cooperative model more. At \(2\,\mathrm{CMC}\) it gives free-monomer concentrations of 0.94 and 0.92 CMC, consistent with the experimentally observed near-plateau, while roughly half the surfactant is micellized. The stepwise model instead has free monomer at 0.73 CMC and only 17.5% in \(n\ge4\), despite experimental micelles containing roughly 62–123 molecules.

For **dimers and trimers below the CMC**, neither endpoint is a measurement:

- the cooperative model’s zero is a lower endpoint imposed by its reaction scheme;
- the stepwise table is a plausible upper envelope, because it assigns the full CMC-derived association energy to every addition step.

Hence, at \(0.5\,\mathrm{CMC}\), for example, the defensible model spread is approximately:

- monomer: **67–100%**;
- dimer: **0–24%**;
- trimer: **0–6.6%**;
- \(n\ge4\): **approximately 0–2.1%**.

Species-resolved \(K_2\) and \(K_3\) measurements would be required to narrow that range.

## Independent checks and experimental evidence

1. **Conservation.** Every row obeys

\[
f_1+f_2+f_3+f_{n\ge4}=1.
\]

For example, the independent mass-action solver at \(2\,\mathrm{CMC}\) SDS returned \(c_1=5.98266\) mM and \(\sum n c_n=16.4000\) mM, exactly reproducing the imposed total.

2. **Alternative large-micelle calculation.** Changing the C12E8 aggregation number across its literature range barely alters the cooperative result, as quantified above.

3. **Experimental sub-CMC evidence.** Pulsed-field-gradient NMR found a marked decrease in SDS self-diffusion in the premicellar region; the authors explicitly analysed the data using monomer, dimer and micelle populations. This demonstrates that “100% monomer” is not exact, although the accessible report does not publish enough numerical equilibrium constants to reconstruct a unique census. [Springer Nature Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S1061933X07040059?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

4. **Why tensiometry cannot provide the census.** Surface tension reports interfacial adsorption and a breakpoint. It does not independently determine \(K_2\), \(K_3\), and the higher association constants; many bulk distributions can therefore reproduce the same breakpoint.

## What transfers to ionic SDS?

Mass conservation and a general mass-action description remain valid. The equal-\(K\) stepwise model does **not** transfer quantitatively because SDS association includes:

- size-dependent electrostatic head-group repulsion;
- diffuse-layer screening;
- Na⁺ binding that changes with aggregate size and concentration;
- strong salt dependence of both CMC and aggregation number.

Modern molecular-thermodynamic treatments therefore include counterion distributions and electrostatic free energy explicitly. [Langmuir](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/la501008x?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Consequently, the SDS stepwise values should be regarded only as a numerical sensitivity scenario. The cooperative table is the better estimate of its gross monomer–micelle partition, while the NMR result establishes that its true sub-CMC dimer fraction is probably nonzero but cannot be calculated uniquely from the published CMC and \(N\) alone.

## Model

I solved the planar Ward–Tordai equation from an initially clean interface,

\[
\Gamma(t)=2c_b\sqrt{\frac{Dt}{\pi}}
-\sqrt{\frac{D}{\pi}}\int_0^t\frac{c_s(\tau)}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}\,d\tau ,
\]

coupled at every timestep to the corrected Frumkin isotherm and equation of state:

\[
(bA_i)c_s=\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}e^{-2a\theta},
\qquad
\gamma=\gamma_0+RT\Gamma_\infty
\left[\ln(1-\theta)+a\theta^2\right],
\quad
\theta=\Gamma/\Gamma_\infty .
\]

This is a diffusion-controlled, local-interfacial-equilibrium model: no unsourced adsorption/desorption rate constant was introduced. Kinoshita et al. used the same Ward–Tordai/Frumkin framework and ionic-activity correction for SDS. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28623702/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For 10 mM total SDS I used an 8.0 mM monomer reservoir, with the excess assigned to micelles. This is explicitly an approximation: published results show that above the CMC micelles can modify monomer adsorption rates even though monomers dominate formation of the interfacial layer. [researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317153002_Adsorption_of_ionic_surfactants_at_microscopic_air-water_interfaces_using_the_Micropipette_interfacial_area-expansion_method_Measurement_of_the_diffusion_coefficient_and_renormalization_of_the_mean_io?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Model

For each droplet diameter \(d\), I solved the rigid-sphere force balance using the finite-Reynolds-number Schiller–Naumann correlation,

\[
C_D=\frac{24}{Re}\left(1+0.15Re^{0.687}\right),
\qquad
\frac{\pi d^3}{6}\Delta\rho g
 =\frac12 C_D\rho_w\frac{\pi d^2}{4}u_\infty^2.
\]

This correlation has about ±5% drag accuracy below \(Re=1000\); here the hindered terminal Reynolds number at \(d_{32}\) is only 0.89. [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/enhanced-settling-and-dispersion-of-inertial-particles-in-surface-waves/676E76A21C80FA7D92207B4A59335EC6?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The 5% dispersed fraction was included through

\[
u(d)=u_\infty(d)(1-\phi)^{4.48},
\]

giving a hindrance multiplier of 0.7947. The exponent \(4.48\pm0.04\) is the experimentally consolidated Richardson–Zaki value for non-Brownian low-Re suspensions. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.09314?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For an initially uniform column,

\[
\boxed{F(t)=\int q_3(d)\min\left[\frac{u(d)t}{H},1\right]\,{\rm d}d}
\]

where \(q_3(d)\) is the oil-volume distribution and \(H=0.10\) m.

The paper describes its distributions as right-skewed, approximately lognormal, with Tween-80 SPAN values between 0.592 and 1.335. It does not provide a machine-readable SPAN specifically for the 330 rpm curve. [researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379563347_Liquid-liquid_two_phase-system_stabilized_by_tween_40_and_80_surfactants_multiparametric_study?utm_source=chatgpt.com) I therefore used:

- central assumption: lognormal, SPAN = 1.000;
- width sensitivity: SPAN = 0.592 and 1.335;
- all cases constrained to the measured \(d_{32}=235\,\mu{\rm m}\).

For a lognormal number distribution,

\[
\sigma_{\ln d}=\frac{\operatorname{asinh}({\rm SPAN}/2)}{1.2815516},
\qquad
\mu_{\ln d}=\ln d_{32}-2.5\sigma_{\ln d}^2.
\]

## Coalescence-rate constraint

I define the constant Smoluchowski kernel \(K\) by

\[
\frac{{\rm d}N}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{K}{2}N^2,
\qquad
N(t)=\frac{N_0}{1+KN_0t/2}.
\]

For a volume-conserving, self-preserving distribution,

\[
\frac{d_{32}(t)}{d_{32}(0)}
 \simeq\left(1+\frac{KN_0t}{2}\right)^{1/3}.
\]

For the paper’s lognormal SPAN range, the initial number concentration derived from \(\phi=0.05\) and \(d_{32}=235\,\mu{\rm m}\) is

\[
N_0=(8.60\times10^9)-(1.50\times10^{10})\ {\rm m^{-3}}.
\]

The Mastersizer reproducibility was better than 1%, while the reported standard deviations ranged up to 10 μm; the authors found no meaningful \(d_{32}\) or DSD change after a month. [researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379563347_Liquid-liquid_two_phase-system_stabilized_by_tween_40_and_80_surfactants_multiparametric_study?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Therefore:

| Nondetection threshold after 30 d | Derived upper bound on \(K\) |
|---|---:|
| 1% in \(d_{32}\), 2.35 μm | \(K<2.72\times10^{-18}\ {\rm m^3\,s^{-1}}\) |
| Worst reported SD, 10 μm | \(\boxed{K<1.20\times10^{-17}\ {\rm m^3\,s^{-1}}}\) |
| Conservative \(2\sigma\), 20 μm | \(K<2.49\times10^{-17}\ {\rm m^3\,s^{-1}}\) |

The boxed value is my operational upper bound. It is conditional on the constant-kernel, no-breakup model; it is not a directly measured rate constant.

Across \(K=0\) to \(1.20\times10^{-17}\ {\rm m^3\,s^{-1}}\), the predicted \(t_{50}\) changes by only about **3 μs**. Even the conservative \(2\sigma\) bound changes it by less than 10 μs. Gravitational separation is therefore complete far too quickly for the month-scale coalescence bound to matter.

This resolves the apparent contradiction: **unchanged droplet size means resistance to coalescence, not resistance to creaming**. Indeed, the paper’s apparatus figure explicitly shows phase separation after stopping the impeller. ## Single falsification experiment

Prepare the exact paper formulation using fresh Tween-80 solution and the paper’s 60 min, 330 rpm Rushton-turbine procedure. Immediately transfer it—within roughly two seconds—to a transparent 10.0 cm-high column at \(25.0\pm0.2^\circ{\rm C}\). Use an internal diameter of at least 25 mm to suppress wall effects.

Record the column at ≥30 frames s\(^{-1}\), backlit, and extract the clarified lower-layer height \(h_c(t)\). Compare

\[
F_{\rm obs}(t)=h_c(t)/10.0{\rm\,cm}
\]

at 5, 10, 15, 30, 60 and 120 s. Simultaneously measure the initial volume DSD and oil density; inserting those two measured inputs removes the largest present uncertainties.

- \(t_{50}\) differs by more than 20%, or
- \(|F_{\rm obs}-F_{\rm model}|>0.10\) at any of the prescribed times.

A measured \(t_{50}\) of minutes or hours would decisively show that an omitted mechanism—most likely flocculation into a space-spanning network, a yield stress, wall adhesion, or strong counter-current hydrodynamic coupling—invalidates the independent-droplet creaming model.

## Basis and interpretation

Cloud point is a temperature, so I interpret “in water at 25 °C” as using the standard HLD reference temperature \(T_\mathrm{ref}=25^\circ\mathrm C\). Because cloud point depends on concentration, the numerical results below are specifically for a **1 wt% aqueous solution of a pure, monodisperse alkyl ethoxylate**. That is the concentration basis of the published cloud-point correlation. [ACS Publications](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.langmuir.5b03064?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

I used the nonionic HLD equation

\[
\mathrm{HLD}=bS-k\,\mathrm{EACN}+c_T(T-25)+C_c,
\]

with a phase balance or cloud point assigned by \(\mathrm{HLD}=0\):

\[
T=25-\frac{bS-k\,\mathrm{EACN}+C_c}{c_T}.
\]

For pure linear \( \mathrm{C_iE_j} \) ethoxylates,

\[
C_c=2.4+0.28i-j.
\]

That group-contribution expression is published for linear alkyl ethoxylates. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1359029420300509?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For cloud-point calculations, the micellar hydrocarbon-like environment is represented by the published apparent \(\mathrm{EACN}_{\rm mic}=7.3\). The other method constants are \(b=0.13\), \(c_T=0.060\ {\rm ^\circ C^{-1}}\), and \(k=0.16\), with the literature range \(k=0.15\)–0.17. Salinity \(S\) is in g NaCl/100 mL. ## Where not to trust these numbers

Do not use these predictions without experimental confirmation for:

- Commercial ethoxylates having broad EO or alkyl-chain distributions; their cloud behavior can differ substantially from a pure \(C_iE_j\) compound. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26467232/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- Concentrations materially different from 1 wt%, especially near concentration-dependent phase boundaries.
- More than eight EO units—C12E8 is already at the published method boundary.
- Mixed surfactants, cosurfactants, alcohols, electrolytes other than ordinary NaCl, or very high salinity.
- Systems where crystallization, lamellar/liquid-crystalline phases, or kinetic emulsification obscure the equilibrium transition.
- Predicting phase-region widths, emulsion stability, or required formulation time: HLD predicts balance, not those quantities. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11857855/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For purchasing decisions, I would treat the bold conservative bands—not the one-decimal central values—as the actionable result.

## Notation and standard states

To avoid the literature’s \(\alpha/\beta\) ambiguity, I use:

- \(\alpha\): fraction of counterions dissociated from the micelle.
- \(b=1-\alpha\): fraction bound to the micelle.

At 298 K,

\[
RT=(8.314462618)(298)=2.47771\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
\]

Thermodynamic logarithms must contain a dimensionless activity; changing from \(c/c^\circ\) to mole fraction changes the numerical standard Gibbs energy because it changes the standard state. [activity (A00115)](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/A00115?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For aqueous SDS,

\[
c_{\rm cmc}/c^\circ=0.0083,
\]

and, using the published water concentration \(55.345\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\) at 25 °C, [ACS Publications](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.chemrev.7b00259?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

\[
x_{\rm cmc}=
\frac{0.0083}{55.345+0.0083}
=1.49946\times10^{-4}.
\]

For the EPR result,

\[
\alpha=0.272\pm0.017,\qquad b=0.728\mp0.017.
\]

The EPR paper explicitly reports \(\alpha=0.272\pm0.017\) for SDS micelles with aggregation numbers below about 110. [ACS Publications](https://pubs.acs.org/jpcbfk/article/105/29/6798/3572805/A-Definition-of-the-Degree-of-Ionization-of-a?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 1. Reproducing Bouchal et al.

Bouchal et al. report for dodecylguanidinium chloride:

\[
m_{\rm cmc}=0.0062\pm0.0003\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}},\quad
\beta=0.74\pm0.01,
\quad
\Delta G^\circ_{\rm mic}=-28.3\pm0.9\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},
\]

where their \(\beta\) is described as the counterion-binding degree. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4783955/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Converting the molality to mole fraction using \(55.5085\) mol water per kilogram,

\[
x_{\rm cmc}
=\frac{0.0062}{55.5085+0.0062}
=1.11682\times10^{-4},
\]

so

\[
RT\ln x_{\rm cmc}
=(2.47771)\ln(1.11682\times10^{-4})
=-22.5488\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
\]

### What reproduces their number

Using

\[
\boxed{\Delta G^\circ_{\rm mic}
=(2-\beta)RT\ln x_{\rm cmc}}
\]

gives

\[
(2-0.74)(-22.5488)
=\boxed{-28.409\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}},
\]

only \(-0.11\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) from their reported \(-28.3\).

Equivalently, because the dissociation degree is \(\alpha=1-\beta=0.26\),

\[
(2-\beta)=(1+\alpha).
\]

Thus their numerical value uses the **mole-fraction pseudo-phase convention with the multiplier \(1+\) counterion dissociation**, not \(1+\) binding.

### Internal inconsistency in the paper

The paper prints

\[
\Delta G^\circ_{\rm mic}=RT(1+\beta)\ln x_{\rm cmc}.
\]

With \(\beta=0.74\), that expression gives

\[
(1+0.74)(-22.5488)
=\boxed{-39.23\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}},
\]

which does not reproduce the table. Therefore, the table and printed equation cannot both use \(\beta\) as the binding degree. The table’s \(-28.3\) is consistent with \((2-\beta)\), or with reinterpreting the multiplier’s variable as the dissociation degree.

Direct propagation of the quoted \(m_{\rm cmc}\) and \(\beta\) uncertainties through the reproducing equation gives approximately

\[
-28.41\pm0.27\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}
\]

by quadrature, smaller than the paper’s reported ±0.9 kJ/mol. The extra uncertainty is therefore not recoverable from the two tabulated input errors alone.

### Finite-aggregation-number mass-action convention

Phillips’ finite-\(N\) mass-action expression, extended to bound counterions, is

\[
\Delta G^\circ_{\rm mic}=
-\frac{RT}{N}\ln\!\left[
\frac{1}{a_+^{\,bN}}
\frac{N-2}{N^2(2N-1)}
\left(
a_{\rm cmc}\frac{2N^2-N}{2N^2-2}
\right)^{1-N}
\right].
\]

The formula and its large-\(N\) pseudo-phase limit are documented in the mass-action treatment originating with Phillips. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamics_of_micellization?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Using the commonly reported SDS aggregation number \(N=62\) at the CMC, [ACS Publications](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ja00486a062?utm_source=chatgpt.com) \(b=0.728\), and \(a_+=a_{\rm cmc}\) at zero added salt gives:

| Mass-action activity scale | Result (kJ/mol) |
|---|---:|
| Mole fraction | **−37.01 ± 0.37** |
| 1-M concentration | **−19.98 ± 0.20** |

The finite-\(N\) correction relative to the corresponding ionic pseudo-phase values is only \(+0.69\) kJ/mol on the mole-fraction scale and \(+0.53\) kJ/mol on the molarity scale. Uncertainty in \(N\) is not included because the cited \(N=62\) source does not provide one.

Using \(\log_{10}\) with a factor 2.303 is not a separate convention: it is algebraically identical to using \(\ln\).

## 3. Spread and literature prevalence

Across all tabulated, literature-substantiated standard-state and counterion conventions:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta G^\circ_{\rm mic}=-11.87\ \text{to}\ -43.63\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}
}
\]

—a total spread of

\[
\boxed{31.76\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
\]

Restricting the comparison to conventions that actually use the supplied EPR ionization result gives \(-15.10\) to \(-37.70\) kJ/mol, still a **22.60 kJ/mol spread**.

In the literature surveyed here, the predominant applied-surfactant convention is:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta G^\circ_{\rm mic,x}
=(1+b)RT\ln x_{\rm cmc}
=(2-\alpha)RT\ln x_{\rm cmc}
}
\]

—that is, mole-fraction standard state plus an explicit counterion correction. Examples use either \(1+b\), where \(b\) denotes binding, or \(2-\alpha\), where \(\alpha\) denotes dissociation. [rsc.org](https://www.rsc.org/suppdata/c5/cp/c5cp02642c/c5cp02642c1.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Direct-molarity formulas occur, particularly in applied corrosion/surfmer literature, but are a minority in this survey. [electrochemsci.org](https://www.electrochemsci.org/papers/vol11/111210978.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For SDS, the most directly comparable value under that predominant convention is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\Delta G^\circ_{\rm mic,x}
=-37.70\pm0.37\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}
\]

conditional on the supplied CMC, with uncertainty from EPR \(\alpha\) alone.

**Recommendation:** Report \(\boxed{\Delta G^\circ_{\rm mic,x}=(2-\alpha)RT\ln x_{\rm cmc}}\), explicitly defining \(\alpha\) as counterion dissociation and reporting \(x_{\rm cmc}\), CMC and \(\alpha\) alongside it, because this is the predominant convention surveyed and eliminates both the hidden standard-state shift and the binding-versus-dissociation ambiguity.

### Prediction method

I used the Klevens homolog relation

\[
\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{CMC}}{\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}}\right)=A-Bn_C ,
\]

a standard semi-empirical relationship for members of one surfactant homologous series. [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2046206923003728?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Only shorter sodium alkyl sulfates were used to determine \(A\) and \(B\):

| Calibration compound | \(n_C\) | Literature CMC |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sodium octyl sulfate | 8 | 0.130 M |
| Sodium dodecyl sulfate | 12 | 0.00830 M |
| Sodium tetradecyl sulfate | 14 | 0.00210 M |

These homolog-series values are reported for water near room temperature. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_micelle_concentration?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Unweighted regression gives

\[
A=1.50294,\qquad B=0.29863,
\]

and hence, without using any C16 datum,

\[
\log_{10}\mathrm{CMC}_{C16}
 =1.50294-0.29863(16)
 =-3.27521,
\]

\[
\mathrm{CMC}_{C16}=10^{-3.27521}
=5.306\times10^{-4}\ {\rm M}.
\]

The fitted slope corresponds to a tail contribution

\[
2.303RTB=1.70\ {\rm kJ\,mol^{-1}}
\]

per added methylene at 298 K, a physically reasonable magnitude for transferring hydrocarbon surface from water into a micellar core.

The factor-of-two interval, \(\pm0.301\) log decade, was assigned *before validation*. It represents approximately one carbon-equivalent uncertainty in the homolog extrapolation and covers deviations caused by counterion binding, activity coefficients, aggregation-number changes, and experimental CMC definitions. It is a model uncertainty, not a statistical 95% confidence interval.

## 3. Post-prediction held-out comparison

Only after freezing the preceding result did I search specifically for sodium hexadecyl sulfate.

Kumar and Kaur measured SHS in water at 298.15 K using conductivity and fluorescence and reported:

|---|---:|

| Mean of the two | **0.2164 mM** |

The study, temperatures, and experimental methods are described in the paper, and the tabulated pure-water values are explicit. [Lookchem](https://www.lookchem.com/FreePDFArticle_85100-78-3_78613630.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Comparison with the frozen prediction:

\[
\frac{0.5306}{0.2164}=2.45,
\]

so my prediction is:

- **0.314 mM too high**
- **+145% relative error**
- **0.390 log decade high**
- a **factor of 2.45** above the held-out experimental break.

The measured value is also just outside the predeclared factor-two interval: 0.216 mM versus its 0.265 mM lower bound. Therefore the uncertainty interval failed, albeit by only about 23% at its lower edge.

### Critical physical qualification

Sodium C16 sulfate has a reported Krafft temperature around **45 °C**, substantially above 25 °C. [citeseerx.ist.psu.edu](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=1c854cee6dd2eccde352a8e5de05d2ebff45e64d\&repid=rep1\&type=pdf\&utm_source=chatgpt.com) Below the Krafft temperature, the equilibrium solubility is normally insufficient to reach a stable micellar phase: hydrated surfactant crystals or precipitate pre-empt equilibrium micellization.

Consequently:

- **0.531 mM is best interpreted as an extrapolated intrinsic/metastable micellization threshold**, assuming the dissolved micellar branch remains accessible.
- At strict equilibrium in pure water at 25 °C, SHS may have **no operational equilibrium CMC** in the ordinary sense; the relevant quantity is first its monomer solubility.
- The published 0.216 mM break is properly called an **apparent CMC under the authors’ preparation and measurement protocol**. It might represent metastable micelles, sub-Krafft aggregates, a solid–aggregate transition, or sensitivity to small amounts of electrolyte or homolog impurity.

The remarkably small quoted instrumental uncertainty does not cover these systematic thermodynamic questions.

## 4. Molecular assumptions and likely failure modes

The prediction assumes:

1. A pure, linear sodium alkyl sulfate with exactly 16 carbons and the same sulfate headgroup chemistry as the calibration homologues.
2. An approximately constant free-energy increment per added methylene.
3. Similar sodium counterion binding and headgroup electrostatics across the homologous series.
4. Pure water, negligible background electrolyte, and concentration expressed on a comparable molar/activity standard.
5. Access to an equilibrium or reproducible metastable micellar branch.
6. No competing crystalline or hydrated-solid phase.

It fails or becomes method-dependent when:

- **Krafft precipitation intervenes**, as it does at 25 °C for sufficiently pure SHS.
- The solution is heated to dissolve the material and then cooled, creating protocol-dependent supersaturation.
- Trace NaCl or especially Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ changes screening, surface activity, or precipitation; classic SHS surface-tension work found strong sensitivity even to small electrolyte concentrations. [ConnectSci](https://connectsci.au/ch/article-pdf/12/1/1/37150/ch9590001.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- The sample contains C14/C18 homologues, hexadecanol, or inorganic salt. Some commercial “sodium cetyl sulfate” materials are explicitly sold as mixed cetyl/stearyl sulfate rather than a pure C16 compound. [1120-01-0 - TCI Chemicals](https://www.tcichemicals.com/OP/en/p/H0080?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- Different methods identify different transitions—conductivity slope break, fluorescence polarity change, surface tension, scattering, or onset of turbidity.
- Chain-length-dependent aggregation number, micelle shape, counterion condensation, or nonideal monomer activity invalidates a constant \(A-Bn_C\) extrapolation.

**Bottom line:** the blind homolog prediction is \(5.31\times10^{-4}\) M, but the held-out apparent experimental value is about \(2.16\times10^{-4}\) M. More importantly, because 25 °C lies below the SHS Krafft temperature, neither should be quoted as an unqualified equilibrium CMC without reporting sample history, dissolved concentration, solid-phase observations, and the measurement criterion.
