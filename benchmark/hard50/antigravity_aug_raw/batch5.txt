## H41: Rubingh Pointwise and Pooled Analysis of Corrupted Binary Series

### Method and Analysis
We evaluate the binary surfactant mixture with pure CMCs:
- $\text{CMC}_1 = 3.0\text{ mM}$
- $\text{CMC}_2 = 3.5\text{ mM}$

For a binary mixture, Rubingh's regular solution theory solves for the micellar mole fraction $x_1$ and interaction parameter $\beta$:
$$\frac{x_1^2 \ln\left(\frac{\alpha_1 \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}}}{x_1 \text{CMC}_1}\right)}{(1 - x_1)^2 \ln\left(\frac{(1 - \alpha_1) \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}}}{(1 - x_1) \text{CMC}_2}\right)} = 1$$
$$\beta = \frac{\ln\left(\frac{\alpha_1 \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}}}{x_1 \text{CMC}_1}\right)}{(1 - x_1)^2}$$

Evaluating each row pointwise:
1. $\alpha_1 = 0.0305, \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}} = 2.541\text{ mM} \implies x_1 \approx 0.2000, \beta \approx -3.1985$
2. $\alpha_1 = 0.1502, \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}} = 1.809\text{ mM} \implies x_1 \approx 0.3500, \beta \approx -3.1996$
3. $\alpha_1 = 0.4615, \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}} = 1.460\text{ mM} \implies x_1 \approx 0.5000, \beta \approx -3.2009$
4. $\alpha_1 = 0.8061, \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}} = 4.086\text{ mM}$: Here $\text{CMC}_{\text{mix}} = 4.086\text{ mM} > \max(\text{CMC}_1, \text{CMC}_2) = 3.5\text{ mM}$. In regular solution theory, mixed micellization requires the chemical potential in mixed micelles to be lower than or equal to pure states; a mixed CMC higher than both pure components is physically impossible. Solving Rubingh's equation yields no root in $(0, 1)$ (`no_solution`).
5. $\alpha_1 = 0.9590, \text{CMC}_{\text{mix}} = 2.202\text{ mM} \implies x_1 \approx 0.8000, \beta \approx -3.2000$

Thus, the row at $\alpha_1 = 0.8061$ is identified as the corrupted point ($n_{\text{points, clean}} = 4$).
Fitting a pooled regression across the 4 clean points yields $\beta_{\text{mean}} = -3.2000$.

## H43: Hydrophilic-Lipophilic Difference (HLD) and AOT Curvature Source Discrepancy

### Method and Analysis
Using the standard HLD equation for an anionic surfactant:
$$\text{HLD} = \ln(S) - k \cdot \text{EACN} + C_c$$
Given $S = 5\text{ wt}\%$ ($\ln(5) = 1.609438$), $\text{EACN} = 10$, $k = 0.16$, $\Delta T = 0$:
$$k \cdot \text{EACN} = 0.16 \times 10 = 1.60$$
$$\ln(S) - k \cdot \text{EACN} = 1.609438 - 1.60 = 0.009438$$

- **Source 1 ($C_c = -0.92$):**
  $$\text{HLD} = 0.009438 - 0.92 = -0.9106$$
  Since $\text{HLD} < -0.3$, this predicts a direct oil-in-water microemulsion (**Winsor I**).
- **Source 2 ($C_c = +2.4$):**
  $$\text{HLD} = 0.009438 + 2.4 = +2.4094$$
  Since $\text{HLD} > +0.3$, this predicts a water-in-oil reverse microemulsion (**Winsor II**).

**Source evaluation:**
AOT (sodium bis(2-ethylhexyl) sulfosuccinate) has a double branched ethylhexyl tail and a compact sulfosuccinate headgroup, making it strongly lipophilic with a strong natural tendency to form reverse micelles and W/O microemulsions in hydrocarbon oils without cosurfactants (positive $C_c$). A value of $C_c = -0.92$ in review articles is a known transcription error copying the value of SDHS (sodium dihexyl sulfosuccinate). Source 2 ($C_c = +2.4$, primary measurement) is the correct value.
