### Question H01

#### Principles and Calculations
1. **Optical Constant $K$**:
   Using the Debye equation for static light scattering:
   $$K = \frac{4\pi^2 n^2 (dn/dc)^2}{N_A \lambda_0^4}$$
   Given:
   - Solvent refractive index $n = 1.333$
   - Refractive index increment $dn/dc = 0.135\text{ mL/g}$
   - Avogadro constant $N_A = 6.02214076 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$
   - Vacuum wavelength $\lambda_0 = 632.8\text{ nm} = 6.328 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}$
   
   $$\lambda_0^4 = (6.328 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm})^4 = 1.6033878 \times 10^{-17}\text{ cm}^4$$
   $$\text{Numerator} = 4\pi^2 (1.333)^2 (0.135)^2 = 39.4784176 \times 1.776889 \times 0.018225 \approx 1.278453\text{ mL}^2/\text{g}^2$$
   $$\text{Denominator} = 6.02214076 \times 10^{23} \times 1.6033878 \times 10^{-17} \approx 9.655828 \times 10^6\text{ cm}^4/\text{mol}$$
   $$K = \frac{1.278453}{9.655828 \times 10^6} = 1.32395 \times 10^{-7}\text{ mol}\cdot\text{cm}^2/\text{g}^2$$

2. **Debye Plot ($K c / \Delta R$ vs. $c$)**:
   $$\frac{K c}{\Delta R} = \frac{1}{M_w} + 2 A_2 c$$
   Computing the ordinates $y_i = K c_i / \Delta R_i$:
   - $c = 0.0020\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 8.9867 \times 10^{-6}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies y = 2.94646 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
   - $c = 0.0040\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 1.7048 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies y = 3.10640 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
   - $c = 0.0060\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 2.4319 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies y = 3.26645 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
   - $c = 0.0080\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 3.0911 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies y = 3.42642 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
   - $c = 0.0100\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 3.6915 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies y = 3.58648 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$
   - $c = 0.0120\text{ g/mL},\ \Delta R = 4.2407 \times 10^{-5}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies y = 3.74643 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$

   Linear regression yields:
   $$\text{Slope} = 2 A_2 = 8.000 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol}\cdot\text{mL/g}^2 \implies A_2 = 4.00 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol}\cdot\text{mL/g}^2$$
   $$\text{Intercept} = \frac{1}{M_w} = 2.78645 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol/g}$$
   $$M_w = \frac{1}{2.78645 \times 10^{-5}} = 35888.0\text{ g/mol}$$

3. **Aggregation Number $n$**:
   $$n = \frac{M_w}{M_0} = \frac{35888.0}{448.6} = 80.0$$

4. **Mass-Action Free Energy of Micellization**:
   $$X = \frac{\text{CMC}}{55.5} = \frac{8.7 \times 10^{-5}}{55.5} = 1.56757 \times 10^{-6}$$
   $$\ln(X) = -13.36623,\quad \ln(n) = \ln(80) = 4.38203$$
   $$\Delta G = R T \left[\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)\ln(X) - \frac{1}{n}\ln(n)\right]$$
   $$\Delta G = 8.314462618 \times 298.15 \times \left[\left(1 + \frac{1}{80}\right)(-13.36623) - \frac{4.38203}{80}\right] \times 10^{-3} = -33.68\text{ kJ/mol}$$

### Question H03

#### Principles and Calculations
1. **Model Selection (Two-segment vs Three-segment)**:
   - Points 1 to 3 ($0.005, 0.010, 0.020\text{ mM}$) show a flat baseline near pure water surface tension ($\gamma \approx 71.8\text{ mN/m}$).
   - Points 4 to 9 ($0.05\text{ to }3.00\text{ mM}$) form the linear premicellar decline against $\log_{10}(c)$.
   - Points 10 to 12 ($6.00, 10.00, 16.00\text{ mM}$) form the postmicellar plateau ($\gamma \approx 31.1\text{ mN/m}$).
   - Comparing by BIC, the three-segment model strongly outperforms the two-segment model ($\Delta\text{BIC} < 0$). Thus, `three_segment_selected` is `true`, with `n_baseline_points = 3`.

2. **CMC Determination**:
   The break occurs between the last declining point ($3.00\text{ mM}$) and the first plateau point ($6.00\text{ mM}$). In $\log_{10}$ space:
   $$\log_{10}(\text{CMC}) = \frac{\log_{10}(3.00) + \log_{10}(6.00)}{2} \implies \text{CMC} = \sqrt{3.00 \times 6.00} = \sqrt{18} \approx 4.24264\text{ mM}$$

3. **Gibbs Prefactor and Surface Excess**:
   - **Gibbs prefactor $n$**: For a 1:1 ionic surfactant in water with **no added electrolyte**, electrical neutrality dictates that both the surfactant amphiphilic ion and the counterion co-adsorb at the interface, and both ions change concentration simultaneously in the bulk solution. Hence, the Gibbs prefactor is $n = 2$.
   - Premicellar decline slope against $\ln(c)$:
     $$\frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C} = \frac{1}{\ln(10)} \frac{d\gamma}{d\log_{10} C} = -8.39846\text{ mN/m} = -8.39846 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N/m}$$
   - Maximum surface excess $\Gamma_{\max}$:
     $$\Gamma_{\max} = -\frac{1}{n R T} \frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C} = \frac{8.39846 \times 10^{-3}}{2 \times 8.314462618 \times 298.15} = 1.69395 \times 10^{-6}\text{ mol/m}^2$$
   - Minimum area per molecule $A_{\min}$:
     $$A_{\min} = \frac{10^{18}}{N_A \Gamma_{\max}} = \frac{10^{18}}{6.02214076 \times 10^{23} \times 1.69395 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.98028\text{ nm}^2$$

4. **Free Energy of Micellization**:
   With $\alpha = 0.31$ and $X = \frac{4.24264 \times 10^{-3}}{55.5} = 7.6444 \times 10^{-5}$:
   $$\Delta G_{\text{mic}} = (1 + \alpha) R T \ln(X) = 1.31 \times 8.314462618 \times 298.15 \times \ln(7.6444 \times 10^{-5}) \times 10^{-3} = -30.78\text{ kJ/mol}$$

### Question H04

#### Principles and Calculations
1. **Multi-Point van 't Hoff Analysis with Curvature ($\Delta C_p \neq 0$)**:
   The Gibbs-Helmholtz relation with constant $\Delta C_p$ referenced to $T_r = 298.15\text{ K}$:
   $$\Delta G_{\text{mic}}(T) = \Delta H(T_r) - T \Delta S(T_r) + \Delta C_p(T_r) \left[(T - T_r) - T \ln\left(\frac{T}{T_r}\right)\right]$$
   where at each temperature $T$:
   $$\Delta G_{\text{mic}}(T) = (2 - \beta) R T \ln\left(\frac{\text{CMC}(T)}{55.5}\right),\quad (2 - \beta) = 2 - 0.268 = 1.732$$
   Fitting $[\Delta H, \Delta S, \Delta C_p]$ across the 8 temperatures via ordinary least squares gives:
   $$\Delta H(298.15\text{ K}) = -2.8464\text{ kJ/mol}$$
   $$\Delta S(298.15\text{ K}) = 115.12\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$$
   $$\Delta C_p = -814.74\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$$

2. **Standard Free Energy at 298.15 K**:
   $$\Delta G_{\text{mic}}(298.15\text{ K}) = 1.732 \times 8.314462618 \times 298.15 \times \ln\left(\frac{0.00974}{55.5}\right) \times 10^{-3} = -37.13\text{ kJ/mol}$$

3. **Thermodynamic Driver**:
   At $298.15\text{ K}$, $-T \Delta S = -298.15 \times 115.12 \times 10^{-3} = -34.32\text{ kJ/mol}$, whereas $\Delta H = -2.85\text{ kJ/mol}$. The entropic term contributes over $92\%$ of the favorable negative free energy change; therefore, micellization is **entropy-driven**.
