I used the surfactant-computation tools, and the structural classifier flagged exactly the ambiguity your question is about — so the connectivity needs to be resolved by hand. Here’s the analysis.

Charge type. The classify_surfactant_charge_type tool returns cationic (high confidence): two quaternary ammonium centers, no anionic groups, with two bromide counterions. The surfactant ion itself carries a net +2 charge, balanced by the 2 Br⁻.

Architecture — the key question. Stripping the two Br⁻ counterions, the molecule is:

(CH₃)₃N⁺–(CH₂)₁₆–N⁺(CH₃)₃

Walking the connectivity:

At each nitrogen: three methyl groups plus one bond into the central chain. Nothing hydrophobic flanks the headgroups — only CH₃ substituents.
Between the two headgroups: a single polymethylene chain of 16 carbons. This one chain is the entire hydrophobic region.
Beyond the headgroups: nothing but methyls — no pendant long alkyl tails.

A genuine gemini has the topology tail–head–spacer–head–tail: two complete amphiphilic units (each a headgroup with its own hydrophobic tail) joined near the headgroups by a short spacer, as in the classic m–s–m dialkyl geminis CₙH₂ₙ₊₁–N⁺(CH₃)₂–(CH₂)ₛ–N⁺(CH₃)₂–CₙH₂ₙ₊₁. There each nitrogen has a tail on one side and the spacer on the other.

This molecule has no such tails. The two charged heads sit at the two ends of a single hydrophobic backbone, with the hydrophobic domain lying between them rather than hanging off the outside. That is the defining signature of a bolaform surfactant (bolaamphiphile) — hydrophilic groups capping both ends of one hydrophobic skeleton — not a gemini.

The classify_surfactant_structural_family tool reported dimeric_gemini_type, but that label rests only on a coarse signal (exactly two strong ionic headgroups + long chain), and the tool itself disclosed in its caveats that this signal cannot separate a true gemini from a bolaform and must be confirmed against the actual connectivity. Having done that, the structure resolves cleanly as bolaform. (The “11 long-chain matches” is just overlapping substructure hits sliding along the one C₁₆ chain — a single chain, not two tails.)

So this is hexane-1,16-bis(trimethylammonium) dibromide — a dicationic bolaform surfactant, not a true gemini.
