(a) Where a pre-CMC isotherm stops matching — qualitatively. A pre-CMC adsorption isotherm (Langmuir–Szyszkowski, or Frumkin if there’s lateral interaction) is constructed to describe only the rising-adsorption branch below the CMC. It will track the data well through the steep sub-CMC descent and then break down in the neighborhood of the CMC — roughly from the knee of the curve onward, i.e. at and above the CMC. On a log-c axis that’s the region where the measured points flatten into the plateau while the fitted line keeps sliding downward.

(b) Why it diverges there — the physics. Below the CMC, essentially all added surfactant exists as free monomer, so bulk monomer activity rises with total concentration and the interface keeps filling — exactly the regime the isotherm models. Once the CMC is reached, two things happen that the isotherm has no term for:

Monomer activity gets clamped. Added surfactant goes into micelles, not into raising free-monomer concentration. The chemical potential driving adsorption is pegged near its CMC value, so the real interface stops changing and γ plateaus. The isotherm, which maps γ to total concentration as if it were all monomer, doesn’t know micelles exist and keeps predicting further γ reduction.
The interface is already saturated. Near the CMC the monolayer is at (or near) maximum packing — surface excess has hit Γ_max — so there’s no additional adsorption to be had regardless of what the bulk does.

There’s also a common second-order divergence right at the low end for ionic or impure systems (activity-coefficient effects, trace-impurity minima), but the dominant, expected failure is the one above: the model omits micellization, so it fails from the CMC upward.
