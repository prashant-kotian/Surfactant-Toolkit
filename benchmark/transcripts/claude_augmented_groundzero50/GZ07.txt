What the SMILES tells us: CCCCCCCCCCCCCCCC[N+](C)(C)C.[Br-] is a C16 (hexadecyl) trimethylammonium cation paired with bromide — a classic single-tail cationic surfactant. From first principles, the fully extended hydrocarbon tail of a C16 chain is only ~2 nm (Tanford: ℓ_max ≈ 0.15 + 0.1265·n_C ≈ 1.67 nm for n=16), so a spherical micelle of this surfactant has a core radius around that length plus a thin headgroup/hydration shell. A hydrodynamic radius of ~1.6 nm is exactly what you’d expect for a small, roughly spherical micelle of this molecule. The DOSY number is physically self-consistent with the chemistry.

The comparison with DLS: The DLS value of ~40 nm is about 25× larger than the DOSY-implied radius. These two numbers are not measuring the same effective “average,” and the gap is almost certainly a weighting artifact rather than one measurement being simply wrong:

DOSY-NMR diffusion is population/number-weighted. It reports the diffusion of the species actually carrying the surfactant’s NMR signal — i.e., the vast majority of the molecules, which sit in the abundant small micelles. So DOSY reports ~1.6 nm because that’s where most of the material is.
DLS intensity is weighted by roughly R⁶ (Rayleigh scattering). A trace population of large objects — vesicles, loose aggregates, or even dust/filtration artifacts at tens of nm — scatters so strongly that it dominates the intensity-weighted size, even at vanishingly small number fraction.

So the most likely reality is a bimodal system: the bulk of the surfactant is in ~1.6 nm micelles (seen by DOSY), coexisting with a small fraction of ~40 nm aggregates that dominate the DLS signal. The two techniques are both “correct” for what they measure; they simply emphasize opposite ends of the distribution.

Caveats worth flagging: it’s worth confirming whether the 40 nm DLS figure is the intensity-weighted or number-weighted radius — if it’s intensity-weighted (the default), the discrepancy is expected and benign; if it’s genuinely number-weighted, that would point to real large structures and merit scrutiny of sample prep/filtration. One should not average or “reconcile” the two values into a single number.
