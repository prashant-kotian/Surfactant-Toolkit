Trend and why it doesn’t mirror the CMC

ΔG_mic decreases monotonically (grows steadily more negative) across the whole range — micellization becomes progressively more favorable as temperature rises, with no turning point.

The raw CMC data, by contrast, is U-shaped: it falls to a minimum near 298.15 K (9.74 mM) and then rises. So the two do not mirror each other.

The reason is in the structure of ΔG_mic = (2 − α)·RT·ln(X_cmc):

The RT prefactor dominates. It climbs monotonically (~12% from 283→318 K) and sets the direction of the trend. ln(X_cmc) is a large negative number (≈ −8.6), so a rising RT multiplying a large negative log drives ΔG steadily downward regardless of what CMC does.
The CMC enters only weakly, and through a logarithm. Across the full range CMC moves only ~9.74→11.47 mM (≈18%), and taking the log compresses that further: ln(X_cmc) swings by barely ~0.16 over the whole table. That is far too small to overturn the RT trend.
The CMC’s shape leaves only a faint fingerprint. As CMC climbs at the high end, the rising ln(X_cmc) partially offsets the rising RT, so the step-to-step decrease in ΔG shrinks (−0.67 kJ/mol per step near the bottom, down to −0.23 near the top). The curve flattens slightly where the CMC turns up, but never reverses — the CMC minimum produces no corresponding extremum in ΔG.

In short: the CMC’s temperature dependence is a second-order modulation on top of a first-order RT trend, so a non-monotonic CMC still yields a monotonic ΔG_mic.
