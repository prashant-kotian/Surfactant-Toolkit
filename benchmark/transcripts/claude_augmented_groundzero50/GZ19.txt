What’s not determinable — and why

The partial specific volume of the whole monomer is

v̄ = V_molar(full molecule) / M = [V_tail + V_headgroup + V_counterion] / M

Tanford’s relation describes only the alkyl tail. For this molecule (sodium dodecyl sulfate) the remaining pieces are the anionic sulfate-ester head (–OSO₃⁻) and the Na⁺ counterion, and their contributions are not recoverable from a chain-volume formula. The reason is physical, not just bookkeeping: ionic groups and small cations in water cause electrostriction — water packs tightly around the charge, so the apparent/partial molar volume of an ion is much smaller than its bare geometric volume and can even be negative (Na⁺ in water is a textbook case of a negative partial molar volume). A geometric alkyl-chain relation carries no information about this.

I deliberately did not call estimate_partial_specific_volume: its signature requires headgroup_molar_volume_cm3_per_mol, and that value is exactly the quantity not provided here.
