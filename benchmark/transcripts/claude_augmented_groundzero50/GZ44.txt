Geometric (Tanford) aggregation number

For n_c = 12, the Tanford relations give a tail volume of 350.2 Å³ and a critical chain length of 16.68 Å. Modeling the core as a sphere of radius equal to that length:

N_agg = (4/3 · π · 16.68³) ÷ 350.2 ≈ 55.5

This is a pure first-principles idealization. It assumes a perfect sphere whose radius is the fully extended chain, a liquid-hydrocarbon core of uniform density, and no headgroups, counterions or water in the volume budget. It uses no measurement at all.

Agreement and why this comparison is different in kind

The two values — 54.5 and 55.5 — agree to within about 2%, which is well inside the combined uncertainty of either approach. That is strong agreement.

What makes it meaningful is that the two numbers are methodologically orthogonal. They share no instrument, no calibration, no fitted parameter, and no common data. One is an experimental observable read off a real scattering measurement; the other is a packing argument built from chain geometry alone. When you instead compare two citations that both used static light scattering on this compound, any agreement is partly structural — they are measuring the same observable with the same technique, inheriting the same systematic assumptions (dn/dc convention, what “the particle” includes, interaction corrections), so consistency is as much a check on reproducibility as on truth. Here, agreement is a genuine cross-validation: an independent theoretical prediction landing on top of an independent measurement corroborates the underlying physical picture — that this micelle really is close to a sphere whose core is filled by tails at roughly their extended length.

Two caveats keep this honest rather than a coincidence worth over-reading. The geometric estimate uses the maximum chain length as the radius, which tends to overfill the sphere and bias N_agg slightly high; real cores run a little smaller because tails are not fully extended. And a spherical model is only appropriate near the CMC for a C12 single-tail cationic — at higher concentration or added salt the aggregate grows and elongates, at which point the spherical geometric number stops being the right comparison. So the close match should be read as “the spherical-micelle model is appropriate for this system under these conditions,” not as a universal validation.
