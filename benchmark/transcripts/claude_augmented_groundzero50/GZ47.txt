All three numbers come from the tool (Szyszkowski/Langmuir, nonionic so n-factor = 1, T = 298 K). The physical validity is a separate question that turns on the CMC, which is ~1e-4 mol/L for this compound (it’s C₁₂E₈).

The equation describes surface tension as a function of monomer activity in the sub-CMC regime. Below the CMC, bulk concentration ≈ monomer concentration, so the equation is on solid ground. At and above the CMC, monomer activity saturates — extra surfactant goes into micelles, not the surface — so the real surface tension plateaus at γ_CMC. Feeding a bulk concentration above the CMC into the equation treats all of it as surface-active monomer, which it isn’t, so the result is unphysical.

5e-6 mol/L (0.05 × CMC) → 51.17 mN/m. Well below CMC. Valid use.

5e-5 mol/L (0.5 × CMC) → 38.68 mN/m. Still below CMC. Valid use.
