ethylene-glycol-type linker to a benzyldimethylammonium head (Cl⁻). Component 2 is dodecyltrimethylammonium bromide (DTAB), a small, simple cationic surfactant (Br⁻). Both carry a positive head, so naïvely you’d expect like-charge repulsion and near-ideal-to-antagonistic mixing (β ≈ 0 or slightly positive).

What the data actually show. Both interaction parameters come out strongly negative, i.e. the mixing is attractive / synergistic despite both components being cationic — and it is markedly asymmetric. A single-β regular-solution (Rubingh) model cannot describe it: the β you’d back out swings from about −1.9 to +0.8 across the composition range, and the values implied by f₁ and f₂ disagree at every point. That internal inconsistency is the signature of asymmetric non-ideality, which the two-parameter asymmetric Margules model (w₁₂ ≠ w₂₁, with RST as its w₁₂=w₂₁ special case) handles exactly.

Fitted parameters (from the linearity of ln f₁/(1−x₁)² in x₁, then verified against every point with the tool):

w₁₂ ≈ −3.56 — a lone molecule of the bulky aromatic surfactant dropped into a DTAB-rich micelle is very favorable.
w₂₁ ≈ −0.89 — a lone DTAB in an aromatic-rich micelle is only mildly favorable.

The model reproduces all five measurements to 3 significant figures (e.g. x₁=0.305 → f₁ 0.393/f₂ 0.652; x₁=0.483 → 0.769/0.427; x₁=0.808 → 1.028/0.286), so the parameters are tightly determined.

Physical picture. The large size/structure disparity is the source of both the synergy and its asymmetry. Inserting the big aromatic/ether molecule into a crowded, highly charged DTAB micelle relieves headgroup charge density and adds van der Waals / cation–π / dipole contacts — a big energy win (very negative w₁₂). The reverse insertion (small DTAB into an aromatic-rich, already loosely packed micelle) gains much less (small w₂₁). Because the two limits differ so much, the excess free energy of mixing is lopsided in composition — exactly why the symmetric model fails and a single β is meaningless for this pair.

f₂ ≈ 0.315 — the minority DTAB remains strongly stabilized (f₂ ≪ 1), consistent with the attractive, asymmetric interaction.
