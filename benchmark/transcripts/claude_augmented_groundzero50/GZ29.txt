The fitted interaction is not physically meaningful. The one-parameter Langmuir/Szyszkowski model already reproduces all nine points across four decades of concentration to within ~0.01–0.04 mN/m (R² = 0.9999999) using the independently measured Γ_max. There is essentially no systematic residual left for a lateral-interaction term to explain, and indeed the Frumkin fit prices that term at a ≈ 0.0046 — i.e. it collapses back onto Langmuir. In the Frumkin/Fowler–Guggenheim convention the pair interaction enters as exp(−2aθ), so a = 0.0046 corresponds to a cohesion energy of order 2a·kT ≈ 0.01 kT per molecule — about 1% of thermal energy. A real attractive (a > 0) or repulsive (a < 0) interaction is O(1); |a| ≥ 2 is required even for 2D condensation. This a sits ~400× below that threshold and is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

Why BIC still “picked” Frumkin. The data are essentially noiseless (residuals at the 7th significant figure). BIC’s complexity penalty is calibrated against the noise level, so when noise → 0 the criterion will “reward” an extra parameter for a microscopic RSS reduction that carries no physical content. The ΔBIC = 4.4 reflects a seventh-figure improvement in fit, not the detection of a cohesive monolayer. For any practical purpose the interface is described by the simple Langmuir/Szyszkowski isotherm with ideal (non-interacting) adsorption.

So: the model-selection step formally returns Frumkin, but the correct physical reading is that C₁₂E₈ shows no meaningful surfactant–surfactant lateral interaction in the pre-CMC regime — the Frumkin term is a degenerate, noise-driven artifact.
