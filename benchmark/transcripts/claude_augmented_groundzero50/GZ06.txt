Electrostatic screening and electrophoretic analysis

Debye screening length. For 100 mM NaCl, the ionic strength is I = ½Σcᵢzᵢ² = 0.100 M (cation and anion each contribute 0.1 M × 1²). At 298.15 K this gives a Debye length of κ⁻¹ = 0.9614 nm — i.e. the diffuse double layer around the micelle decays over roughly 1 nm, noticeably thinner than the micelle itself.

Debye parameter × radius (κa). Taking the micelle radius as the stated a = 2.637 nm:

κa = a / κ⁻¹ = 2.637 nm / 0.96137 nm = 2.743

This places the particle squarely in the intermediate double-layer regime. The screening length and the particle radius are of the same order (a ≈ 2.7 κ⁻¹), so neither limiting electrophoresis theory is valid: the Hückel limit requires κa ≪ 1 (thick, point-like double layer) and the Smoluchowski limit requires κa ≫ 1 (thin double layer, typically κa ≳ 100). At κa ≈ 2.7 we are between them.

Mobility correction factor (Henry’s function). The correct treatment at intermediate κa is Henry’s function f(κa) in μ = (2εζ/3η)·f(κa), which interpolates smoothly between the Hückel value f = 1 and the Smoluchowski value f = 1.5. I evaluated it by passing a single fixed mobility through the Hückel regime (f ≡ 1) and through the full-range Henry approximations, recovering f as the ratio ζ_Hückel/ζ_fullrange. The three full-range forms give:

Approximation	f(κa = 2.743)
Qin	1.057
Ohshima	1.085
Swan–Furst	1.094

I adopted the Swan–Furst value, f ≈ 1.094, because it is the most accurate of the three across the full κa range (<0.1% vs. the exact Henry function). I confirmed this independently against the exact Henry series (the 1 + (κa)²/16 − 5(κa)³/48 − … + [(κa)⁴/8 − (κa)⁶/96]·e^{κa}E₁(κa) expansion), which evaluates to ≈ 1.096 at this κa — consistent with Swan–Furst and ~1% above the Ohshima fit.

Why Henry and not a relaxation-corrected model. The relaxation treatments (Ohshima–Healy–White, O’Brien–White) are the right choice only when the double layer is thin and the surface is highly charged (|ζ| ≳ 50 mV), where counterion relaxation makes mobility grow sub-linearly in ζ. Those models are explicitly restricted to κa ≳ 10; at κa = 2.74 the particle is well below their domain of validity, and nothing in the problem indicates a high zeta potential. The appropriate, physically justified correction factor here is therefore the standard (low-field, relaxation-free) Henry function.
