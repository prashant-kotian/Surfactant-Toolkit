Γ_max ≈ 2.37 × 10⁻⁶ mol·m⁻² (maximum surface excess)
A_min ≈ 0.70 nm² (minimum area per molecule at the air–water interface)

That area is physically sensible for a trimethylammonium head group whose footprint is set by electrostatic head-group repulsion. Note this rests on the n = 2 choice: if the real solution actually contained excess inert salt, n = 1 would halve Γ_max and double A_min. Nothing in a SMILES-plus-curve can decide that, so I’ve taken the no-added-salt reading the dataset implies.

A Langmuir-vs-Frumkin isotherm model selection was not done — only 3 points sit in the pre-CMC decline, below the ≥4 needed to fit and compare models. Γ_max and A_min above don’t depend on that fit (they come straight from the slope), so they stand.

Micellization thermodynamics — what can’t be determined, and why

This is where the data genuinely stops short, in two independent ways:

ΔG_mic — not rigorously determinable (reported null). For an ionic surfactant the micellization free energy is ΔG_mic = (1 + β)·RT·ln(X_cmc), where β is the degree of counterion (Br⁻) binding to the micelle and X_cmc is the CMC mole fraction. That (1 + β) factor is not a small correction — it scales the whole result. β cannot be extracted from a surface-tension curve plus a structure; it needs an independent measurement (conductometry’s pre/post-CMC slope ratio, or an ion-selective/ potentiometric method). To show the sensitivity from first principles: X_cmc ≈ 0.01637/55.5 ≈ 3.0 × 10⁻⁴, so RT·ln(X_cmc) ≈ −20 kJ/mol. The actual ΔG_mic therefore lies anywhere from about −20 kJ/mol (β = 0) to −40 kJ/mol (β = 1) depending entirely on a binding degree you haven’t supplied — a factor-of-two window. I won’t collapse that to a single number by assuming β.

ΔS_mic — impossible from this dataset (reported null). Entropy of micellization requires either the temperature dependence of the CMC (ΔS_mic = −∂ΔG_mic/∂T, i.e. ΔG at several temperatures) or an independently measured ΔH_mic (van’t Hoff or ITC) to combine with ΔG. You’ve given data at a single temperature (296.35 K). With one temperature there is no slope to take and no enthalpy to pair, so neither ΔH_mic nor ΔS_mic exists here — and since ΔG_mic itself is already blocked by the missing β, ΔS is doubly out of reach.

To actually get these: add a conductometric β (unlocks ΔG_mic), and measure the CMC at a few more temperatures or run calorimetry (unlocks ΔH_mic and hence ΔS_mic).
