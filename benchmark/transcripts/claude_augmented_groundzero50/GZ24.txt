Is the Maeda free-energy expression appropriate here? No.

The expression ΔG_M = RT(B₀ + B₁X₁ + B₂X₁²), with B₀ = ln(X_cmc2), B₂ = −β, and B₁ = ln(X_cmc1/X_cmc2) − B₂, is Maeda’s model, and it is derived specifically for an ionic + nonionic binary pair. Its whole structure assigns the electrostatic / counterion free-energy contribution to a single ionic component mixing with a neutral partner: B₁ carries that one-sided head-group electrostatic term, and B₀ anchors the free energy to one ionic reference CMC.

In this system both components are ionic (carboxylate + sulfate, each with Na⁺ counterions). The single-ionic-component assumption is violated — the model has no term for the second charged head group’s electrostatics or for the counterion entropy of a two-ionic mixture. You can push the numbers through the algebra mechanically, but the resulting ΔG would not correspond to the physics of this ionic/ionic pair, so I am not reporting it as a valid value.

Appropriate alternatives for an ionic/ionic mixture would be the Rubingh/regular-solution ΔG of mixing (which I’ve used above for X₁ and β) or an electrostatics-aware treatment (e.g. a PB/molecular-thermodynamic model) rather than the Maeda ionic–nonionic form.
