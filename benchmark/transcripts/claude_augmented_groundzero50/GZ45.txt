Compound: sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) — linear C₁₂ chain, sulfate headgroup, Na⁺ counterion. CMC ≈ 8.2 mM, salt-free, 25 °C.

The crux is that for an ionic surfactant the aggregation number is not a single constant — it’s a function of the counterion atmosphere, and therefore of total surfactant concentration. So the two numbers are measured at genuinely different points on the same growth curve.

The mechanism, quantified from the server:

Condition	Free small-ion ionic strength	Debye length κ⁻¹
near CMC (~8.2 mM, all monomeric)	8.2 mM	3.36 nm
69 mM total	~12.9 mM (Na⁺_free ≈ 21.7 mM, DS⁻_free ≈ 4.0 mM, β = 0.728)	2.68 nm

At 69 mM the free-monomer concentration has actually dropped to ~4.0 mM, but the counterion loading is far higher: with β = 0.728 (Bales 2001), ~47 mM of Na⁺ is condensed on micelles and ~22 mM remains free. The net effect is a shorter screening length (3.36 → 2.68 nm, and effectively shorter still if the full counterion reservoir is counted).

Walking the molecular-thermodynamic chain: stronger screening → weaker headgroup–headgroup electrostatic repulsion → smaller equilibrium area per headgroup a₀ → larger packing parameter P = v/(a₀·l_c) → larger equilibrium aggregation number. This growth is monotonic in concentration and well-documented for SDS.

Reconciling the numbers: 69 mM is ~8.5× the CMC — squarely in the growth regime, yet still far below the salt-free sphere-to-rod transition (several hundred mM), so micelles remain globular and N ≈ 63 is a physically sensible globular value. The literature range (44.8–54.2, mean ~49.5) carries no stated concentration and is a basket of techniques; those values are consistent with measurements at or near the CMC, where the double layer is least screened and micelles are smallest. A single higher-concentration point sitting above a near-CMC range is exactly the ordering the physics predicts — not a contradiction. (Inter-technique systematic scatter of 10–30% between SANS, fluorescence quenching, and light scattering reinforces this, but the concentration dependence alone is sufficient.)

A genuine conflict would require the higher-concentration measurement to come out lower, which would run against the screening argument. That is not the case here.
