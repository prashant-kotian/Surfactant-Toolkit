They disagree badly, and not from numerical noise. Look at the raw CMC data: it falls from 9.93 mM (283 K) to a minimum of 9.74 mM (298 K), then rises to 11.47 mM (318 K). That U-shape means the sign of d(ln X_cmc)/dT flips around 298–303 K, and since ΔH_mic ∝ −T²·d(ln X_cmc)/dT, the enthalpy flips sign with it:

Below the minimum, micellization is slightly endothermic (+1.6 kJ/mol) — entropy-driven, the classic hydrophobic-effect signature.
Above the minimum, it’s strongly exothermic (−14.9 kJ/mol).
The wide-span value (−5.3) is just a meaningless average that straddles the CMC minimum — it reports a single number for a quantity that changed sign across the interval.

The gap between (b) and (c) of ~16 kJ/mol over a 20 K separation of interval-midpoints implies a large negative heat capacity of micellization (ΔCp on the order of −0.8 kJ/mol·K). That is exactly the regime where the van’t Hoff tool’s own stated assumption — ΔH constant over the interval — breaks down, so the constant-ΔH two-point method is not self-consistent here.
