Assumptions and limitations
Tanford relations assume a saturated, unbranched, fully extended alkyl chain at maximum extension (a rigid-rod idealization); real chains are conformationally flexible, and l_c is an upper bound rather than a typical length.
Carbon count convention: all 12 tail carbons are treated as hydrophobic. If the carbon bonded to the sulfate oxygen is instead counted as part of the headgroup region, n_c would drop to 11 and both V and l_c would shrink — the problem specifies n_c = 12, which is what I used.
A_min is an experimental input, not derived here, and it is condition-specific (temperature, ionic strength, counterion, degree of counterion binding). CPP scales inversely with it, so the prediction is only as good as that number.
Proximity to the boundary: P = 0.470 sits close to the 1/2 cutoff. A headgroup area a few Ų smaller (tighter packing, e.g. added salt screening the sulfate charge) would push P past 0.5 into the vesicle/bilayer regime; a larger area would move it toward spheres. The morphology call is therefore not robust to modest changes in A_min.
CPP is a geometric packing heuristic, not a free-energy calculation. Actual equilibrium aggregate shape also depends on surfactant concentration, salt, and temperature, which this model does not capture. For a short-to-medium C12 tail the spherical-core reasoning still applies, so no short-chain validity caveat was flagged.
