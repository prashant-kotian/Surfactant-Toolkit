Pre-CMC adsorption behavior

Below the CMC, monomers adsorb at the air–water interface, which is what drives the surface tension down. The appropriate quantities come from the Gibbs adsorption isotherm applied to the premicellar slope of γ vs ln C:

Premicellar slope (dγ/d ln C): −16.88 mN·m⁻¹
Maximum surface excess, Γ_max: 3.43 × 10⁻⁶ mol·m⁻² — using the ionic, no-added-salt prefactor (n = 2), which is the correct choice here because SDS is a 1:1 ionic surfactant and no swamping electrolyte was present. Minimum area per molecule, A_min: 0.485 nm².

An A_min of ~0.48 nm² indicates a fairly loosely packed monolayer, consistent with the electrostatic head-group repulsion you expect among charged sulfate groups with no salt to screen them — reasonable for SDS.

One caveat worth flagging: the dataset provides only two genuinely pre-CMC points, so a formal Langmuir-vs-Frumkin isotherm model selection was not attempted (that needs ≥4 points). Γ_max and A_min above are read directly from the premicellar slope and don’t depend on that model fit, but the slope itself rests on few points, so treat these two numbers as sound in form but modest in precision.
