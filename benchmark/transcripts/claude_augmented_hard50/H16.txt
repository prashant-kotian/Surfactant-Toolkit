Results

CMC = 4.20 mM. A two-segment linear fit on linear concentration finds the break point where the premicellar and postmicellar lines intersect. The fit is essentially exact (R² = 1.00 on both segments), with 4 points below and 8 above the break.

β = 0.72. From the slope ratio: slope below CMC = 0.550 mS·cm⁻¹·mM⁻¹, slope above = 0.154, so β = 1 − 0.154/0.550 = 0.72. (The slope-ratio interpretation of β is standard and widely reported, though its physical basis has been questioned for some systems — treat it as the conventional value.)

ΔG_mic = −30.11 kJ/mol. Using ΔG = (2 − β)·R·T·ln(X_cmc) with X_cmc = CMC/55.5 = 7.57×10⁻⁵, (2 − β) = 1.28, T = 298.15 K.

ΔS_mic = not determinable (null). ΔS requires ΔH via ΔS = (ΔH − ΔG)/T, and ΔH_mic cannot be extracted from a single-temperature conductivity isotherm. You would need either the temperature dependence of the CMC — the CMC (and β) measured at several temperatures spanning 298.15 K, giving ΔH_mic = −(2 − β)RT²·d(ln X_cmc)/dT via a van’t Hoff slope — or a direct calorimetric ΔH_mic from isothermal titration calorimetry. The data given fixes the state only at 298.15 K, so the entropy is underdetermined.
