Both independent methods landed on essentially the same CMC, and the thermodynamics follow cleanly.

Results

CMC from conductivity: 5.75 mM (two-segment linear break-point; both segments fit with R² = 1.000, slopes 0.640 below / 0.220 above)
CMC from tensiometry: 5.74 mM (5.745 mM from the three-segment break-point fit)
Agreement within 2%: The relative difference is |5.75 − 5.745| / 5.747 ≈ 0.0095% — far below the 2% threshold → true
Counterion binding degree β: 0.656, from β = 1 − (slope_above / slope_below) = 1 − (0.220 / 0.640)
ΔG_mic: −30.56 kJ/mol, using (2 − β) = 1.344, CMC = 0.00575 mol/L, and the mole-fraction reference (÷55.5)

The two techniques agreeing this tightly is a good consistency check — conductivity reads the break where ionic mobility drops, tensiometry where the interface saturates, and they coincide. The strongly negative ΔG_mic reflects favorable micellization, with the (2 − β) factor accounting for counterion condensation on the micelle surface.
