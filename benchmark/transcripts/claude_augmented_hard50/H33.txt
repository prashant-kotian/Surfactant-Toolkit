Using the surfactant-computation tool (velocity converted from 0.30 ft/day to 1.0583×10⁻⁶ m/s):

Capillary number: Ca = μv/γ = (12.0 cP × 1.0583×10⁻⁶ m/s) / (32.0 mN/m) = 3.969×10⁻⁷

The tool classifies this as a capillary-dominated regime, where residual oil stays trapped.

Exceeds threshold? No. 3.969×10⁻⁷ is well below Ca* = 1×10⁻⁴ (about 250× below it), so false.

Velocity to reach Ca = 1×10⁻⁴: Since Ca scales linearly with velocity, I scale the Darcy velocity by the ratio of target to current Ca:

v_needed = 0.30 ft/day × (1×10⁻⁴ / 3.969×10⁻⁷) ≈ 0.30 × 251.97 ≈ 75.59 ft/day

That’s a ~252-fold increase over the current rate — illustrating why, in practice, enhanced oil recovery relies on surfactants to drive interfacial tension toward ultra-low values rather than on raising flow velocity.
