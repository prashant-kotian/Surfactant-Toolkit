AOT is sodium bis(2-ethylhexyl) sulfosuccinate: one compact sulfosuccinate head carrying two bulky, branched 2-ethylhexyl tails. The geometry is the whole story:

Twin branched tails give a large tail volume v relative to the effective head area a, pushing the critical packing parameter P = v/(a·l_c) toward and past unity.
P ≳ 1 means the surfactant natively prefers negative (oil-side) interfacial curvature — which is exactly why AOT spontaneously forms water-in-oil microemulsions and bilayer/lamellar phases with no cosurfactant. That is its defining, textbook behaviour.
