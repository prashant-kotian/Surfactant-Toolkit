(a) Where the fit stops matching

The fitted pre-CMC isotherm tracks the measured data well through the low- and mid-concentration rising region, then diverges in the knee region just below and at the CMC — typically within roughly half a decade (a factor of ~3–5) of the CMC, and completely above it. In that zone the measured curve bends over and flattens into the plateau, while the fitted isotherm keeps predicting a continued (or only slowly saturating) decrease in surface tension. So the mismatch is concentrated at the onset of the plateau / the CMC knee, not in the dilute regime.

(b) Why the divergence is physically expected

A pre-CMC adsorption isotherm models a single equilibrium: surfactant monomers adsorb at the air–water interface, and the model equates bulk monomer activity with total surfactant concentration. That assumption is fine while essentially all surfactant is monomeric. Near the CMC, however, micellization kicks in: added surfactant partitions into micelles, so the monomer activity (chemical potential) becomes nearly constant — it is buffered by the micelle–monomer equilibrium. The interface is already near saturation, so surface tension plateaus. A fit that ignores micelle formation has no mechanism for this buffering, so it over-predicts the surface excess and keeps driving surface tension down past the point where the real data flatten. Secondary effects — cooperative/non-Langmuir adsorption, non-ideal solution behavior, and trace impurities — also sharpen the real knee relative to the smooth fitted curve, widening the discrepancy exactly there.
