To determine the HLB value for the surfactant (C12E6) using the modified Davies' method with the Guo, Rong & Ying (2006) effective-chain-length corrections, we evaluate the contributions step-by-step.

**1. Calculate the Effective Counts**
Based on the surfactant's structure, we have an actual tail count of 11 $CH_2$ groups, 1 terminal $CH_3$ group, and an actual head-group count of 6 EO units.

* **Effective $CH_2$ count (alkyl tail):**

$$\text{Effective } CH_2 = 0.965 \times (11) - 0.178 = 10.437$$


* **Effective EO count:**

$$\text{Effective EO} = 13.45 \times \ln(6) - 0.16 \times (6) + 1.26$$


$$\text{Effective EO} = 13.45 \times 1.791759 - 0.96 + 1.26$$


$$\text{Effective EO} = 24.099165 - 0.96 + 1.26 = 24.399165$$



**2. Determine the Group Contributions**
Before applying the Davies weights, we apply these effective counts to the structure.

* **Alkyl Tail (Lipophilic):**
The tail effectively consists of 10.437 $CH_2$ groups and 1 terminal $CH_3$ group.

$$\text{Tail Contribution} = (10.437 \times 0.475) + (1 \times 0.475) = 4.957575 + 0.475 = 5.432575$$


* **EO Head-Group (Hydrophilic and Lipophilic):**
Each of the effective EO units ($-CH_2-CH_2-O-$) contains 1 ether-oxygen and 2 $CH_2$ groups.
* *Hydrophilic (Ether-Oxygen) Contribution:* $24.399165 \times 1 \times 1.3 = 31.718915$
* *Lipophilic ($CH_2$) Contribution:* $24.399165 \times 2 \times 0.475 = 23.179207$
* *Net Head-Group Contribution:* $31.718915 - 23.179207 = 8.539708$


*(Note: This perfectly matches scaling the effective EO count by the net Davies weight of a single EO unit: $1.3 - (2 \times 0.475) = 0.35$. Thus, $24.399165 \times 0.35 = 8.539708$)*

**3. Compute Final HLB**
Using Davies' base formula ($HLB = 7 + \sum \text{Hydrophilic} - \sum \text{Lipophilic}$):


$$HLB = 7 + 8.539708 - 5.432575$$

$$HLB = 10.107133$$

Rounding to two decimal places, the HLB is 10.11.

**Answer:** 10.11

To compute the standard Gibbs free energy of micellization ($\Delta G^\circ_{mic}$), we use the provided equation:


$$\Delta G_{mic} = \text{counterion\_factor} \times R \times T \times \ln(X_{CMC})$$

**1. Identify the given parameters:**

* **Counterion factor:** $1.0$ (fully-bound case)
* **Ideal gas constant ($R$):** $8.31446 \text{ J/(mol K)}$
* **Temperature ($T$):** $303.15 \text{ K}$
* **Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC):** $0.0057 \text{ M}$

**2. Convert CMC to mole fraction ($X_{CMC}$):**
In standard surfactant thermodynamics, the mole fraction of the surfactant in an aqueous solution is calculated by dividing the molar CMC by the molarity of water (commonly approximated as $55.5 \text{ mol/L}$ for dilute solutions).


$$X_{CMC} = \frac{\text{CMC}}{\text{Molarity of water} + \text{CMC}}$$

$$X_{CMC} \approx \frac{0.0057}{55.5 + 0.0057} = \frac{0.0057}{55.5057} = 0.000102692$$


*(Note: Approximating $X_{CMC}$ simply as $\frac{0.0057}{55.5}$ or using a slightly different water molarity based on standard density yields virtually the exact same final rounded value).*

**3. Calculate the standard Gibbs free energy of micellization:**


$$\Delta G_{mic} = 1.0 \times 8.31446 \text{ J/(mol K)} \times 303.15 \text{ K} \times \ln(0.000102692)$$

$$\Delta G_{mic} = 2520.529 \text{ J/mol} \times (-9.18376)$$

$$\Delta G_{mic} = -23147.93 \text{ J/mol}$$

Converting this to kilojoules per mole (dividing by 1000):


$$\Delta G_{mic} = -23.148 \text{ kJ/mol}$$

**Answer:** -23.148

To find the cloud point using the Hydrophilic-Lipophilic Deviation (HLD) framework, we set $\text{HLD} = 0$ since the cloud point represents the phase transition boundary in a surfactant-water system (where the micelle core acts as the equivalent "oil" phase).

The given HLD equation for nonionic ethoxylate surfactants is:


$$\text{HLD} = b \cdot S - k \cdot \text{EACN}_{\text{app}} + C_c + c_T(T - 25)$$

**1. Identify the given parameters and universal constants:**

* **$S$ (salinity)** = $2.5 \text{ g/100 mL}$
* **$b$ (salt constant)** = $0.13 \text{ (g/100 mL)}^{-1}$
* **$k$ (EACN coefficient)** = $0.17$ (This is the universal standard constant for nonionic surfactants in the HLD framework)
* **$\text{EACN}_{\text{app}}$** = $7.3$
* **$c_T$ (temperature coefficient)** = $0.06 \text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$
* **$\text{SACN}$ (alkyl tail carbons)** = $10$
* **$\text{EON}$ (ethylene oxide units)** = $5$

**2. Calculate the Characteristic Curvature ($C_c$):**


$$C_c = 0.28 \cdot \text{SACN} + 2.4 - \text{EON}$$

$$C_c = 0.28(10) + 2.4 - 5$$

$$C_c = 2.8 + 2.4 - 5 = 0.20$$

**3. Substitute the values into the HLD equation and set it to 0:**


$$0 = (0.13 \cdot 2.5) - (0.17 \cdot 7.3) + 0.20 + 0.06(T - 25)$$

$$0 = 0.325 - 1.241 + 0.20 + 0.06(T - 25)$$

$$0 = -0.716 + 0.06(T - 25)$$

**4. Solve for the Temperature ($T$), which is the cloud point:**


$$0.06(T - 25) = 0.716$$

$$T - 25 = \frac{0.716}{0.06}$$

$$T - 25 = 11.9333...$$

$$T = 36.9333... \text{ }^\circ\text{C}$$

Rounding to two decimal places, the estimated cloud point is **36.93** $^\circ\text{C}$.

To compute the Equivalent Alkane Carbon Number (EACN) for octylbenzene based on the provided logic:

1. **Count the side-chain carbons:** The SMILES string `c1ccccc1CCCCCCCC` indicates a benzene ring attached to a fully saturated, unbranched octyl group (8 carbons).
2. **Apply the rule:** The rule states that the aromatic ring contributes 0 to the EACN, and the side chain contributes a value equal to its carbon count. Thus, the EACN is simply the length of the side chain, which is 8.
3. **Identify the rule:** In surfactant science and the Hydrophilic-Lipophilic Deviation (HLD) framework, the empirical observation that the benzene ring contributes 0 to the EACN and the overall EACN equals the side-chain carbon number is known as the **Cash rule** (named after Cash et al., 1977).

**Answer:** 8.0, Cash rule
