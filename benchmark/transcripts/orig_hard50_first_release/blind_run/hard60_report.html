Rectification pass, 2026-10-04

Five questions turned out to be unfair as originally written — not hard, broken. Q31 and Q34 restated the toolkit's own formula incorrectly (verified against source: the toolkit uses a direct multiplier and a CMC‑specific van't Hoff sign convention neither question text carried over correctly). Q17, Q46, and Q59 named a correction/correlation/rule without actually stating it, requiring recall instead of derivation — including a bug in the first fix of Q46 itself (the HLD formula was given with its k coefficient left as a bare symbol, no number supplied; every model had to guess, 0.15–1.0). All five were corrected and every model re-run. Once k=0.16 was actually stated, all 5 models landed on the exact gold (35.72°C) with zero disagreement. Q19 (CTAB literature Cc) was retired outright — it has no derivable answer from any given data, so it was never a fair test of reasoning to begin with. All numbers in this report below reflect the corrected, re-run results.

Two "universal fails" that were actually benchmark bugs

fixed, re-run, now passing

On two questions, all five models independently produced the same wrong-vs-gold answer. That pattern — convergence with each other, divergence from the gold — was the signature of a flawed question, not five unrelated reasoning errors. Checked by hand: in both cases, the literal formula given in the unaugmented question text didn't match what the toolkit itself computes. Both were corrected and re-run; see the per-question matrix below for the new results.

Q31Gibbs free energy of micellization

The question stated ΔG = (1+counterion factor)·RT·ln(x) with counterion factor = 1.0, i.e. a ×2 multiplier — and every model applied that literally.

RT·ln(x) = −23.146 kJ/mol ← matches toolkit gold (−23.148) (1+1)·RT·ln(x) = −46.293 kJ/mol ← matched all 5 models before the fix

The toolkit's real gold is the un-doubled value. Fixed wording now states counterion_factor as a direct multiplier (1.0 = fully bound). Re-run: 4/5 now pass; DeepSeek was the only one to already hit gold exactly even before the fix (full precision carried through, no early rounding).

Q34van't Hoff enthalpy

The question gave the standard sign convention d(ln CMC)/d(1/T) = −ΔH/R and asked to solve it directly — which is exactly what every model did.

literal formula, as stated = +23.55 kJ/mol ← matched all 5 models before the fix toolkit's own gold = −23.55 kJ/mol ← same magnitude, opposite sign

Same magnitude, opposite sign, on every run. Fixed wording now states the CMC-specific convention (no leading minus sign, since CMC is inversely related to the equilibrium constant). Re-run: 5/5 pass.

The adversarial pair

Q53 · Q56

Two questions were deliberately built around inputs that make the toolkit itself report an honest failure (converged=False) — an under-resolved population balance, and an MCMC chain with good R̂ but effective sample size under the toolkit's own threshold. The test isn't the precision of the accompanying number; it's whether the model reports the honest failure or talks itself into a confident, wrong "yes."

Q53Population balance — coarse discretization

5 / 5 models correctly said non-convergent. Every model recognized that 10 size classes can't resolve this coalescence process over 600 s and reported real, if numerically varied, mass error (0.22–1.00 vs. gold 0.987).

Q56MCMC — effective sample size below threshold

Only 2 / 5 (Claude, Gemini) correctly said non-convergent. Qwen and DeepSeek both reported converged=True — exactly the predicted failure mode: assuming "R̂ looks fine" implies convergence, without checking ESS separately. ChatGPT declined to answer rather than commit either way.

Universal near-misses vs. universal wide misses

Failing a tight tolerance isn't the same kind of failure everywhere. Some questions, every model lands within a hair of the gold; others, every model is off by a large, consistent factor — evidence the toolkit's exact numerical method (not just precision) isn't something these models can reconstruct from a plain-language description.

Near-misses — all 5 close, all 5 fail on strict tolerance

Q16 Ward–Tordai adsorption: gold 2.8566, all 5 models land at 2.8568–2.8571 (within 0.0005).
Q27 Debye length: gold 2.3307 nm, all 5 at 2.3029–2.3030 — a consistent −1.2% bias, likely a shared ε(T) approximation.
Q28 Zeta potential: gold 40.386 mV, all 5 within 0.1 mV — almost certainly an εr = 78.4 vs. 78.5 rounding choice.

Wide misses — genuine toolkit-specific gaps

Q08 Maeda free energy: gold −26.976, models scatter from −17.0 to −18.7 — no consensus, no model close.
Q40 DLS z-average size: gold 132.695 nm (regularized NNLS inversion), all 5 land at 34–36 nm — a simple cumulant fit gives a genuinely different answer than the toolkit's ill-posed-inversion solver, exactly as that question was designed to test.

Q17 used to belong here (gold 33.29, models ranged 3.55–11.68) — that was the Guo-correction formula never being stated in the question. With the formula now given, 4/5 models hit 33.29 exactly; the one miss (Gemini) was a self-inflicted overthink, not a capability gap.
