3. Structural Family – Gemini (Dimeric) Bis-Quaternary-Ammonium Surfactant
Key structural evidence:
Two hydrophobic tails (each C₁₆) attached to two separate ionic head-groups within a single molecule.
The two head-groups are covalently linked by a short hydrocarbon spacer (–(CH₂)₆–), which is the hallmark of the gemini (a.k.a. dimeric or twin-tailed) surfactant architecture, first systematised by Zana, Menger, and co-workers.
In the standard shorthand this is a 16-6-16 gemini (two C₁₆ chains, C₆ spacer) with quaternary ammonium (QAC) head-groups.
This contrasts with a conventional single-chain QAC (e.g., CTAB) which has only one tail and one head-group.
4. HLB via Davies' Group-Contribution Method
Davies' formula: HLB = 7 + Σ(hydrophilic group numbers) − Σ(lipophilic group numbers)
Why it cannot genuinely be applied here:
No validated Davies group number for –N⁺(CH₃)₂ Br⁻. Davies' original tables (1957) and subsequent standard compilations assign hydrophilic numbers to –SO₄⁻Na⁺ (38.7), –COO⁻Na⁺ (19.1), –OH (1.9), –O– (1.3), etc., but do not provide a reliable, universally accepted contribution for a quaternary ammonium with a halide counterion.
Gemini architecture is outside the method's scope. Davies' method was parameterised for single-head-group surfactants. A molecule with two ionic head-groups joined by a spacer does not map onto the additive single-head-group scheme without arbitrary doubling, and the spacer's partial burial at the interface is not captured by simple –CH₂– counting.
Even a naive attempt (using a tentative quaternary-N value ≈ 9.4 per head) would give HLB ≈ 7 + 2(9.4) − 42(0.475) ≈ 5.9, but this number is not trustworthy given points 1 and 2.
Therefore, an honest Davies HLB cannot be reported.
