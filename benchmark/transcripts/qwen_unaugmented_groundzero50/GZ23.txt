Symmetric architecture: Tail–Head–Spacer–Head–Tail, the hallmark of gemini (dimeric) surfactants, as opposed to bolaamphiphiles (head–tail–head with a single continuous chain).
Valid HLB Estimation Method
Griffin's method (HLB = 20 × M_hydrophilic / M_total) was derived for nonionic surfactants whose hydrophilic portion is a clearly defined, uncharged polyether (e.g., PEO) block. For a permanently charged quaternary ammonium head, the concept of a "hydrophilic mass fraction" is not physically meaningful in the way Griffin intended. Not valid here.
Davies' group-contribution method (HLB = 7 + Σ hydrophilic group numbers − Σ lipophilic group numbers) was explicitly designed to extend HLB to ionic surfactants by assigning empirical group numbers to charged head groups (including quaternary N⁺) and to each –CH₂–/–CH₃ (lipophilic, ≈ −0.475 per group). Because this molecule is ionic, Davies' approach is the only classical HLB method whose parameterization covers quaternary ammonium groups. One can, in principle, sum the contributions of the two N⁺ centers, the four N-methyl groups, the 28 methylene units, and the two terminal methyls. This is the valid method, though one should note it was parameterized on simpler monomeric surfactants, so applying it to a gemini architecture is an extrapolation.
No other standard HLB method (e.g., the Griffin nonionic formula, or the "saponification-number" variant for esters) is applicable.
