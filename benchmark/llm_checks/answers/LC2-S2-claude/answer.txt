A valid root in (0,1) exists for all three pairs, so this is not a “no_solution” case.

Does the printed result follow?

No. Two independent problems:

Sign of β is wrong. In every pair the mixed CMC lies below even the lower of the two pure CMCs (0.04 < 0.17; 0.06 < 0.10; 0.07 < 0.10). That is the signature of a strong negative deviation, which in Rubingh’s regular-solution theory forces β < 0. The calculation confirms this (β ≈ −8.7, −6.4, −2.6). The printed values are positive, which would mean antagonism — the opposite of what the data show.
Internal contradiction in the paper. The text correctly calls the behavior a “negative deviation / synergic effect,” but a negative deviation corresponds to negative β, not the positive β values tabulated. The printed numbers contradict the paper’s own interpretation as well as the data. (The qualitative conclusion “synergism” is actually right; the β values printed to support it are not — and the effect is strong, not “mild,” for the first two pairs.)

The computed x₁ (~0.49 for all three) also does not match the printed x_M (0.40/0.39/0.35).
