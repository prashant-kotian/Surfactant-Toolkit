(a) A_min from the printed Γ_max. Applying eq (5) to Γ_max = 22.96 μmol/m² = 2.296×10⁻⁵ mol/m²:

A_min = 1/(N_A·Γ_max) = 7.232×10⁻²⁰ m² = 0.0723 nm²

This reproduces the paper’s printed water-row value (0.0723 nm²) exactly, so the A_min entry is internally consistent.

(b) CPP from eqs (6)–(8) with that A_min. With n_C = 10: V = 27.4 + 26.9·10 = 296.4 Å³; l = 1.5 + 1.265·10 = 14.15 Å. Eq (8) specifies A_min in Å², so 0.0723 nm² = 7.232 Å²:

CPP = V/(A_min·l) = 296.4 / (7.232 × 14.15) = 2.90 (dimensionless)

Does the printed result follow?

No. The A_min follows from the data, but the CPP and the morphological conclusion do not.

The printed CPP of 269.809 (and the whole 255–328 band across the seven systems) is a unit error. Those three-digit numbers appear only if A_min is left in nm² while V and l are in Å³ and Å — i.e. eq (8)’s nm²→Å² conversion (a factor of 100) is skipped. Feeding A_min = 0.0723 as if it were Å² gives 289.6, which is the order of the printed values; using the correct 7.232 Å² gives 2.90. CPP is a bounded, dimensionless geometric ratio, so a value in the hundreds is not physically interpretable at all.

Beyond the arithmetic, the inferred shape does not follow either. Spherical micelles require CPP ≤ 1/3; the dimensionally correct value here is 2.90, which predicts inverted structures, not spheres. So neither the printed CPP nor the corrected CPP supports a “globule → spherical” assignment. Two further warning signs sit upstream: Γ_max = 22.96 μmol/m² is itself anomalously large (roughly 5–10× typical surfactant values), which is what drives the unphysically small A_min of 0.07 nm² (≈7 Å², far tighter than a real headgroup can pack); and with n_C = 10 the Hartley–Tanford long-chain reasoning behind CPP is already at the edge of validity. The chain of logic rests on a suspect input and a units mistake.
