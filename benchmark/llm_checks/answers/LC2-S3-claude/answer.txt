Rubingh’s regular-solution model gives two equations for a binary mixture:

β = ln(X₁c / x₁c₁) / (1 − x₁)²
β = ln(X₂c / x₂c₂) / (1 − x₂)², with x₂ = 1 − x₁

Eliminating β leaves one self-consistency equation in x₁; β then follows. The bulk mole fractions X₁ come from the 1:1-by-weight preparation, X₁ = M₂/(M₁+M₂): 0.7434 (P184/P188), 0.8129 (P184/P407), 0.6000 (P188/P407).

Does the printed result follow?

No. Two independent problems:

Sign of β is wrong. In Rubingh’s convention, negative β = synergism, positive β = antagonism. The data show every mixed CMC lying far below both pure CMCs and far below the Clint ideal value (for P184/P188, ideal ≈ 0.35 mM vs. measured 0.04 mM). A mixed CMC well below ideal is a strong negative deviation and forces strongly negative β. The computed values (−8.68, −6.44, −2.57) reflect this. The printed table gives positive β (+3.68, +3.23, +1.72), which would mean antagonism — the opposite of what the same data imply. The paper’s own text (“negative deviations… synergic effect”) contradicts the positive sign it tabulates. The sign error holds regardless of whether X₁ is taken from weight (used here) or as equimolar, since the large CMC depression dominates.
Magnitude and x₁ also disagree. The true deviation is strong, not “mild” (β reaches ≈ −8.7), and the micellar mole fractions are ≈ 0.49 for all three, not 0.40/0.39/0.35.

So the printed β and xᴹ are not what the printed CMCs yield; the qualitative claim of synergism is directionally right, but the reported β values carry the wrong sign (and wrong magnitude), and x₁ differs.
