To determine the surfactant's pre-CMC adsorption behavior from first principles, we can cross-examine the Gibbs adsorption isotherm with equations of state for the Langmuir and Frumkin models.

**1. General Physical Constants & Limits**
Given $T = 298$ K and the ideal gas constant $R \approx 8.314$ J/(mol·K):
$RT = 2477.57$ J/mol

The independently measured maximum surface excess is $\Gamma_{max} = 2.21 \times 10^{-6}$ mol/m$^2$.
We can calculate the scaling factor $RT\Gamma_{max}$:
$RT\Gamma_{max} = 2477.57 \times 2.21 \times 10^{-6} = 5.475 \times 10^{-3}$ J/m$^2 = 5.475$ mN/m

**2. Isotherm Equations of State**
The surface pressure is defined as $\Pi = \gamma_0 - \gamma$, where $\gamma_0 = 72.0$ mN/m.

* **Langmuir Model**: Assumes no lateral interactions between adsorbed molecules. Its equation of state (the Szyszkowski equation) is:
$\Pi = -RT\Gamma_{max} \ln(1 - \theta)$
Where $\theta = \Gamma / \Gamma_{max}$ is the surface coverage fraction.
* **Frumkin Model**: Accounts for lateral interactions through parameter $a$ (where positive $a$ indicates net attraction). Its equation of state is:
$\Pi = -RT\Gamma_{max} [\ln(1 - \theta) + a\theta^2]$

**3. Data Verification against the Langmuir Model**
Let's select points to test if a non-zero Frumkin interaction parameter $a$ is necessary, or if the Langmuir model is sufficient. We can extract the apparent coverage $\theta_{Gibbs}$ from the slope of the experimental data and compare it to the theoretical $\theta_{EOS}$ required to produce the observed surface pressure.

*Test Interval: $c = 3.00 \times 10^{-5}$ to $8.00 \times 10^{-5}$ mol/L*

The coverage derived from the Gibbs thermodynamic slope matches the predicted coverage of the Langmuir equation of state almost perfectly at both low and high limits. This dictates that the additional $a\theta^2$ term in the Frumkin equation is completely unnecessary (i.e., $a \approx 0$).

These two forces cancel out, resulting in a net-zero lateral interaction parameter at the interface, mimicking ideal Langmuir-like behavior.
