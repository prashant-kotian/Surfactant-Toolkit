### 2. Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC)

To find the CMC, we examine the pre-micellar linear decrease in surface tension ($\gamma$) as a function of the logarithm of concentration ($\log_{10}C$), and locate its intersection with the post-micellar baseline.

### 3. Pre-CMC Adsorption Behavior

The surface excess concentration ($\Gamma_{max}$) and the minimum area per molecule ($A_{min}$) at the air-water interface are determined using the **Gibbs adsorption isotherm**:


$$\Gamma = -\frac{1}{n R T} \frac{d\gamma}{d\ln C}$$


Because this is a 1:1 ionic surfactant in pure water (no added electrolyte), both the surfactant ion and its counterion must adsorb to maintain electroneutrality, meaning **$n = 2$**.

* The slope $d\gamma/d\ln C = -7.12 \text{ mN/m}$.
* Using $R = 8.314 \text{ J/(mol K)}$ and $T = 298.15 \text{ K}$, the maximum surface excess is **$\Gamma_{max} = 1.44 \times 10^{-6} \text{ mol/m}^2$**.
* Converting this to the minimum area per molecule ($A_{min} = 1 / (N_A \Gamma_{max})$) yields **$A_{min} = 1.16 \text{ nm}^2$**. This relatively large area per molecule is physically consistent with the bulky, double-tailed (twin 2-ethylhexyl) hydrophobic structure of the surfactant.

### 4. Free Energy of Micellization ($\Delta G_{mic}$)

For an ionic surfactant, the standard free energy of micellization using the phase separation model is given by:


$$\Delta G_{mic} = (1 + \beta) R T \ln X_{cmc}$$


where $\beta$ is the degree of counterion binding to the micelle and $X_{cmc}$ is the mole fraction of the surfactant at the CMC.
Surface tension data alone is insufficient to calculate $\beta$ (which typically requires conductometry or specific ion electrode measurements). Because we lack the counterion binding parameter, $\Delta G_{mic}$ **cannot genuinely be determined** from the given dataset.
