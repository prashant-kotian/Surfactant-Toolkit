To analyze the properties of the surfactant micelle from first principles, we break the calculations down into the core geometry, packing parameters, and hydrodynamic size.

### 1. Surfactant Geometry & Morphology

Using Tanford's standard empirical relations for a saturated alkyl chain with $n_c = 12$ carbons:

* **Tail volume ($V$):**
$V = 27.4 + 26.9(12) = 350.2 \, \text{\AA}^3$
* **Critical chain length ($l_c$):**
$l_c = 1.5 + 1.265(12) = 16.68 \, \text{\AA}$
* **Critical packing parameter ($P$):**
$P = \frac{V}{a \cdot l_c} = \frac{350.2}{44.70 \times 16.68} \approx 0.4697$
* **Morphology:**
Since $1/3 < P \le 1/2$, the preferred geometry is a **cylindrical/rodlike micelle**.

### 2. Micelle Core Axial Ratio

Given the aggregation number $N_{agg} = 63$ and the prolate ellipsoid model where the minor semi-axis $b = l_c$:

* **Micelle core volume ($V_{core}$):**
$V_{core} = N_{agg} \times V = 63 \times 350.2 = 22,062.6 \, \text{\AA}^3$
* **Major semi-axis ($a$):**
$V_{core} = \frac{4}{3} \pi a b^2$
$22,062.6 = \frac{4}{3} \pi \cdot a \cdot (16.68)^2 \implies a \approx 18.931 \, \text{\AA}$
* **Axial ratio ($p$):**
$p = \frac{a}{b} = \frac{18.931}{16.68} \approx 1.135$

### 3. Hydrodynamic Radius (Naive vs. Perrin-Corrected)

The experimental diffusion coefficient is $D = 9.3 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{m}^2/\text{s}$, temperature $T = 298.15 \, \text{K}$, and viscosity $\eta = 0.8903 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$.

* **Naive Stokes-Einstein Radius ($R_{h,naive}$):**
Assuming a perfect sphere:
$R_{h,naive} = \frac{k_B T}{6 \pi \eta D} = \frac{1.3806 \times 10^{-23} \cdot 298.15}{6 \pi (0.8903 \times 10^{-3}) (9.3 \times 10^{-11})} \approx 2.6375 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{m} = 2.6375 \, \text{nm}$
* **Perrin Friction Correction ($F$):**
A prolate ellipsoid experiences more drag than a sphere of the equivalent volume ($R_0$). The shape correction factor $F(p)$ for $p = 1.135$ is:
$F(p) = \frac{\sqrt{p^2 - 1}}{p^{1/3} \ln(p + \sqrt{p^2 - 1})} \approx 1.00143$
* **True Volume-Equivalent Radius ($R_0$):**
$R_0 = \frac{R_{h,naive}}{F(p)} = \frac{2.6375}{1.00143} \approx 2.6338 \, \text{nm}$

**Correction Direction & Reasoning:**
The shape-corrected (true) volume-equivalent radius is **smaller** than the naive estimate. Because an ellipsoid has a larger surface-area-to-volume ratio than a sphere, it generates higher frictional drag. A perfect sphere would need to be artificially bloated to a larger volume (the naive radius) to exhibit the same sluggish diffusion as the more streamlined but drag-heavy ellipsoid.
