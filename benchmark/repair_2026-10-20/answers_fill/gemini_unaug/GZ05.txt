# Delta H = R * d(ln X) / d(1/T)

    return R * (ln_X[j] - ln_X[i]) / (inv_T[j] - inv_T[i])

To determine the thermodynamics of micellization for hexadecanesulfonic acid (SMILES: `CCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCS(=O)(=O)O`) from the provided Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC) values, we must analyze the temperature dependence of the CMC using the van't Hoff (Gibbs-Helmholtz) relationship.

### 1. Enthalpy of Micellization ($\Delta H_{mic}$)

From first principles, the van't Hoff equation relates the equilibrium constant of micellization to the apparent enthalpy of micellization. For a surfactant, this is expressed as:


$$\frac{d(\ln CMC)}{d(1/T)} = \frac{\Delta H_{mic}}{R}$$


*(Note: Because this is a derivative of a natural logarithm, any multiplicative constant used to convert CMC into standard state units—such as converting mM to mole fraction by dividing by 55,508 mM—vanishes. Thus, $\Delta H$ is independent of the choice of standard concentration state).*

Integrating between two temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ yields:


$$\ln\left(\frac{CMC_2}{CMC_1}\right) = -\frac{\Delta H_{mic}}{R} \left(\frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_1}\right)$$


Where $R = 8.314 \text{ J/(mol K)}$. Let's calculate $\Delta H_{mic}$ using the given data (temperatures must be in Kelvin):

* $T_1 = 30.0^\circ\text{C} = 303.15\text{ K}$, $CMC_1 = 0.592\text{ mM}$
* $T_2 = 50.0^\circ\text{C} = 323.15\text{ K}$, $CMC_2 = 0.805\text{ mM}$
* $T_3 = 70.0^\circ\text{C} = 343.15\text{ K}$, $CMC_3 = 1.11\text{ mM}$

**Calculations:**

* **30 °C to 50 °C:**
$\Delta H = -8.314 \times \frac{\ln(0.805 / 0.592)}{(1/323.15 - 1/303.15)} = -12,516 \text{ J/mol} = \mathbf{-12.52 \text{ kJ/mol}}$
* **50 °C to 70 °C:**
$\Delta H = -8.314 \times \frac{\ln(1.11 / 0.805)}{(1/343.15 - 1/323.15)} = -14,810 \text{ J/mol} = \mathbf{-14.81 \text{ kJ/mol}}$
* **Widest Span (30 °C to 70 °C):**
$\Delta H = -8.314 \times \frac{\ln(1.11 / 0.592)}{(1/343.15 - 1/303.15)} = -13,592 \text{ J/mol} = \mathbf{-13.59 \text{ kJ/mol}}$

**Consistency Check:**
The adjacent temperature pairs yield values that differ by ~18% (-12.52 vs -14.81 kJ/mol). They do not perfectly agree. This lack of self-consistency under a constant-enthalpy van't Hoff assumption reveals a non-zero heat capacity of micellization ($\Delta C_{p,mic} \approx -114 \text{ J/mol K}$). This is physically expected due to the hydrophobic effect, which causes the van't Hoff plot to curve rather than form a perfectly straight line.

### 2. Free Energy ($\Delta G_{mic}$) and Entropy ($\Delta S_{mic}$)

These properties **cannot be genuinely determined** from the given information alone, for two primary reasons:

1. **Missing Counterion Binding Degree ($\beta$):** Hexadecanesulfonic acid is an ionic surfactant. The true standard free energy of micellization for an ionic surfactant is $\Delta G_{mic}^\circ = (1 + \beta)RT \ln(X_{cmc})$. Without experimental data (e.g., from conductivity measurements) to determine $\beta$ (the fraction of counterions bound to the micelle), the true magnitudes of $\Delta G$ and $\Delta S$ are unknown.
2. **Standard State Dependence:** Unlike enthalpy, calculating $\Delta G = RT \ln(CMC)$ does not utilize a derivative, meaning the result is highly sensitive to standard state unit assumptions. Using molarity vs. mole fraction yields drastically differing free energy and entropy values.
